## 📊 Revenue, profit and cash

### Three words people mix up

- **Revenue (sales):** all money customers pay you.
- **Profit:** what is left **after all costs**.
- **Cash:** the money you actually have to spend today.

> **Example:** you sell 100 bags at 5,000 each = **500,000 revenue**. If the bags cost you 300,000 and other costs (transport, rent, data) are 80,000, your **profit is 120,000**.

> 📌 **Remember:** revenue is not profit, and profit is not cash.

::: check Sales 500,000, costs 380,000. What is the profit?
- 500,000
+ 120,000
- 380,000
= 500,000 minus 380,000 is 120,000.
:::

### Two kinds of costs

- **Fixed costs:** the same each month (rent, salaries, subscriptions).
- **Variable costs:** change with sales (materials, packaging, delivery).

### Why cash and profit differ

A customer who **buys on credit** adds to revenue today but pays later, so profit looks good while **cash is empty**. Also, **buying stock** uses cash before it becomes profit.

::: check Why can a profitable business run out of cash?
+ Customers pay late, or money is tied up in stock
- Profit and cash are always equal
- It cannot happen
= Cash timing matters.
:::

## 🏷️ Pricing for profit

### Know your cost per unit

Add everything it takes to make or deliver **one** item:

> **Example: one cake**
> Ingredients 3,500 + packaging 500 + gas and power 700 + delivery 800 = **5,500 cost**.

### Add your profit margin

If you want a **40% margin** on the price, you need the price to be such that profit is 40% of it. A simple way to start: **cost + markup**.

- **Markup 50%:** price = cost x 1.5 = 5,500 x 1.5 = **8,250**.
- **Profit per cake:** 8,250 minus 5,500 = **2,750**.

> 📌 **Remember:** markup is on cost, and margin is on price. Know which one you are using.

::: check Cost is 4,000. With a 50% markup, what is the price?
- 4,500
+ 6,000
- 8,000
= 4,000 x 1.5 = 6,000.
:::

### Count your own time

Include **your time** and the **overheads** (rent, data, transport, tools) in the price, or you are working for free.

### Check against the market

Compare with competitors, but **do not copy a price that does not cover your costs**. Compete on quality, speed, service or niche.

::: check What should you include in the price besides materials?
+ Your time and overheads
- Nothing
- Only the profit
= If they are left out, the business loses money.
:::

## 🧮 Break-even point

**Break-even** is the number of sales you need so that **revenue equals costs**. After that, each sale brings profit.

### The simple formula

**Break-even units = fixed costs / (price - variable cost per unit)**

> **Example:** fixed costs 60,000 a month. Price 8,000. Variable cost 5,000. Profit per unit = 3,000.
> Break-even = 60,000 / 3,000 = **20 units a month.**

> 📌 **Remember:** if you cannot realistically sell your break-even number, change price, costs or the plan.

::: check Fixed costs 90,000. Price 10,000, variable cost 4,000. Break-even units?
- 6
+ 15
- 90
= Profit per unit is 6,000, and 90,000 / 6,000 = 15.
:::

### Use it to decide

- **Can I raise the price?**
- **Can I cut variable costs** (buy in bulk, change supplier)?
- **Can I lower fixed costs** (cheaper space, share tools)?

::: check Which does NOT help you lower your break-even point?
- Reducing fixed costs
- Raising price safely
+ Selling fewer products at the same cost
= Selling fewer units does not lower the number you need.
:::

## 🧾 Simple records that save you

You do not need fancy software. You need **consistent records**.

### Keep three records

1. **Sales record:** date, customer, item, amount, paid or owing.
2. **Expense record:** date, what, amount, who you paid. **Keep receipts**.
3. **Stock record:** what you have, what you bought, what is left.

Use a notebook, a **free spreadsheet**, or a simple app. Update **daily or weekly**, never monthly from memory.

> 📌 **Remember:** if it is not written down, it did not happen. Records show profit, protect you from theft, and help you get loans.

::: check Why keep business records?
+ To know your real profit and protect the business
- Only to impress the bank
- They are not important
= Records guide every decision.
:::

### Track who owes you

Write down **customers who owe you** and **suppliers you owe**. Follow up on due dates, and avoid giving credit to people with a poor payment record.

### Monthly check

Once a month: **total sales, total costs, profit, cash at hand, debts owed to you and by you**. Ten minutes a month saves months of confusion.

::: check How often should you update your sales record?
- Once a year
+ Daily or weekly
- Only when you are audited
= Fresh records are accurate.
:::

## 💧 Cash flow: the lifeblood

**Cash flow** is money moving in and out of the business over time.

### A simple cash flow table

| Month | Cash in | Cash out | Net | Closing cash |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Start | | | | 50,000 |
| Jan | 300,000 | 280,000 | +20,000 | 70,000 |
| Feb | 200,000 | 260,000 | -60,000 | 10,000 |

> 📌 **Remember:** a business can be profitable on paper and still fail because it runs out of cash. Plan the cash months ahead.

::: check Opening cash 70,000, cash in 200,000, cash out 260,000. Closing cash?
- 130,000
+ 10,000
- 70,000
= 70,000 + 200,000 - 260,000 = 10,000.
:::

### Ways to improve cash flow

1. **Collect faster:** ask for a **deposit** or full payment upfront, invoice quickly, follow up on due dates.
2. **Pay smart:** negotiate longer supplier terms where you can, without damaging trust.
3. **Hold less stock:** do not tie cash in slow items.
4. **Keep a reserve:** one to three months of fixed costs.
5. **Plan for slow seasons.**

::: check Which helps cash flow?
+ Collecting deposits and following up quickly on payments
- Giving unlimited credit
- Buying lots of stock that sits unsold
= Faster inflow and a lean stock help.
:::

## 🏦 Separate your money

### The most common mistake

Using **one pocket** for personal and business money makes it impossible to know if the business makes a profit.

### The fix

1. **Open a separate account** (and wallet) for the business.
2. **Pay yourself a fixed salary or draw** on set dates.
3. **Never** take cash from sales for personal use without recording it.
4. **Record** every transfer between you and the business.

> 📌 **Remember:** pay yourself a fixed amount. The rest belongs to the business.

::: check Why pay yourself a fixed amount?
+ So you can see the business's real profit and plan
- To spend less
- Because it is the law only
= Predictable pay keeps books clear.
:::

### Keep a business reserve

Do not let your **personal emergency fund** and the **business reserve** mix. Each needs its own purpose and balance.

::: check What is a good rule for personal and business money?
+ Keep them in separate accounts, and record transfers
- Mix them for simplicity
- Use only cash
= Separation gives clarity.
:::

## 🌱 Funding and growing safely

### Where money can come from

1. **Your own savings or profit** (the safest, with no repayment).
2. **Family and friends** (put terms in writing).
3. **Cooperatives and thrift groups.**
4. **Grants and business programmes** (research carefully, and beware of fake ones that charge fees).
5. **Bank and microfinance loans.**
6. **Investors**, who take part ownership.

### Loans: use with care

- Know the **total cost**: the interest rate, fees and **repayment dates**.
- Borrow **only for something that earns more than the loan costs**.
- Avoid **instant loan apps** with very high rates and aggressive collection.
- Make sure the **repayment fits your cash flow**, not just your best month.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** if someone asks you to **pay a fee to receive a grant or loan**, it is very likely a scam.

::: check Which is a safe rule about borrowing?
+ Borrow only for something that earns more than the loan costs
- Borrow whatever is offered
- Never read the terms
= Make debt productive and affordable.
:::

### Growing

Grow **step by step**: test a new product with a small batch, track results, and reinvest part of your profit. Scale what works, and stop what does not.

::: check What is a safe way to try something new in a business?
+ Test with a small batch and track results
- Spend all your savings at once
- Copy the biggest competitor blindly
= Small tests reduce the cost of mistakes.
:::

## 🧰 Business finance cheat sheet
