## 🧱 What is cryptocurrency, really?

### Digital money with no central owner

Normal money (naira, dollars) is issued by a **central bank** and held in **banks**. Cryptocurrencies run on a **network of computers** with rules written in code. There is no bank in the middle, and usually **no one to call** if you make a mistake.

### Blockchain in plain words

A **blockchain** is a **shared record book** copied across many computers. New transactions are grouped into **blocks**, linked in a chain, and almost impossible to alter afterwards.

> 📌 **Remember:** a blockchain is a shared, hard-to-alter record of who owns what.

::: check What is a blockchain?
+ A shared record book copied across many computers
- A bank vault
- A social media app
= Everyone on the network keeps the same record.
:::

### Types of crypto

- **Bitcoin:** the first and best-known cryptocurrency, often compared to "digital gold".
- **Other coins and tokens:** thousands exist, and **most lose value or disappear**.
- **Stablecoins:** designed to track a currency such as the dollar. They are **not risk-free**, and some have failed.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** a coin appearing on an app does not make it valuable or safe.

::: check What is true about thousands of smaller coins?
+ Many lose value or disappear
- All grow
- All are backed by banks
= Be very careful with unknown coins.
:::

### Why people are drawn to crypto, and what the catch is

| Attraction | The catch |
|---|---|
| Fast, global transfers | Mistakes cannot be reversed |
| No bank needed | No bank to help if it goes wrong |
| Big price rises in the past | Equally big falls, and scams everywhere |
| Open to everyone | Anyone can launch a coin, including scammers |

::: check Why can crypto transfers be dangerous for beginners?

- They are insured by banks
= Check everything before you send.
:::

## 👛 Wallets, keys and recovery phrases

### What is a wallet?

A **wallet** does not hold coins like a purse. It holds the **keys** that prove you own coins on the blockchain.

- **Public address:** like your account number. Safe to share to **receive** money.
- **Private key or recovery (seed) phrase:** like your PIN and master key together. **Whoever has it controls your funds.**

> 📌 **Remember:** never share your private key or recovery phrase. Not with support staff, friends, or "recovery services".

::: check Who should know your recovery phrase?
+ Only you, kept safe offline
- Customer support
- A friend
= Anyone who has it can empty your wallet.
:::

### Two kinds of wallet

- **Exchange wallet (custodial):** the exchange holds the keys for you. Easy, but you depend on the company.
- **Self-custody wallet (non-custodial):** you hold the keys. Full control and **full responsibility**. Lose the phrase and the money is gone forever.

### Scenario: the "helpful support agent"

You post a question about a failed transfer on social media. A new account replies: *"I am from support. Send me your recovery phrase so I can fix your wallet."*

::: check What do you do?
+ Never share it, report the account, and contact the official support only through the real website
- Send it, since they sound helpful
- Send half of it
= Real support never needs your recovery phrase.
:::

### Protect your recovery phrase

1. **Write it on paper**, never a screenshot, email or cloud note.
2. **Store copies in safe, separate places.**
3. **Never type it into a website** unless you are restoring your own wallet in an app you trust.
4. **Never photograph it.**

::: check Where should you keep your recovery phrase?
+ Written down offline in a safe place
- In your email
- In a screenshot
= Digital copies can be stolen.
:::

## 🏦 Exchanges, fees and how people buy

An **exchange** is a platform to buy and sell crypto.

### Before you use any exchange

1. **Check regulation:** is it **licensed or recognised** in your country? Rules in Nigeria have changed over time, so check **current** guidance from the **SEC** and the **central bank**.
2. **Check its history:** security record, reviews, how long it has operated.
3. **Check the fees:** trading fees, withdrawal fees and the gap between buy and sell prices (the **spread**).
4. **Check your payment method:** use secure, traceable payments.
5. **Turn on two-step verification** straight away.

> 📌 **Remember:** if an exchange fails or is hacked, you might not get your money back.

### What do fees do to a small trade?

```playground-js Challenge: change the amount to 5000 and the fee to 3. What share of your money goes on the way in and out?
const amount = 50000;
const buyFeePercent = 1.5;
const sellFeePercent = 1.5;
const spreadPercent = 1;   // gap between buy and sell price

const afterBuy = amount * (1 - buyFeePercent / 100);
const afterSpread = afterBuy * (1 - spreadPercent / 100);
const afterSell = afterSpread * (1 - sellFeePercent / 100);

console.log("You put in :", amount.toLocaleString());
console.log("You get back (if the price did not move):", Math.round(afterSell).toLocaleString());
console.log("Lost to costs:", Math.round(amount - afterSell).toLocaleString());
```

> 📌 **Remember:** the price must **rise** enough just to cover the costs.

::: check If the price does not move, what happens after buying and selling with fees?
+ You get back less than you put in
- You get back exactly the same
- You always profit
= Fees and spreads are a cost.
:::

### Peer-to-peer (P2P) trading

You buy from or sell to another person. **Risks:** fake payment proofs, chargebacks, scam sellers, and accounts frozen after suspicious transfers. Use platforms with **escrow**, and follow this rule:

> **Never release crypto until the money has truly arrived in your own account.**

### Scenario: the screenshot

A buyer sends a screenshot saying "Payment sent, please release now!" Your bank app shows no new money.

::: check What do you do?
+ Do not release. Wait until the money shows in your own account
- Release, because the screenshot looks real
- Release half
= Screenshots are easy to fake.
:::

## 🎢 Why prices swing so wildly

### Price is driven by belief and supply

Most coins have **no company profits and no government backing** behind them. Prices move on **news, hype, fear, large holders and rules**. This causes **volatility**: fast, large moves up and down.

### Try the recovery calculator

```playground-js Challenge: try a 30%, 50%, 70% and 90% fall. What gain is needed each time?
const fall = 50; // percent your money falls
const left = 100 - fall;
const gainNeeded = (100 / left - 1) * 100;

console.log(`After a ${fall}% fall you hold ${left}% of your money.`);
console.log(`You need a ${gainNeeded.toFixed(0)}% gain just to get back to even.`);
```

> 📌 **Remember:** a 50% loss needs a 100% gain to recover. A 90% loss needs a 900% gain.

::: check A holding drops 50%. What gain is needed to get back to the start?
- 50%
+ 100%
- 25%
= Halved money must double.
:::

### Emotions are the enemy

- **FOMO** (fear of missing out) makes people buy at the top.
- **Panic** makes people sell at the bottom.
- **"Everyone is getting rich"** is a classic warning, not a signal.

### The loss-limit rule

Decide **in advance** the most you can afford to lose entirely, and **never exceed it**.

```playground-js Challenge: change your monthly income and the share you are willing to risk. How much is it in naira?

  console.log("Stop: build your emergency fund first. Risk amount: 0");

  const risk = monthlyIncome * maxShareToRiskPercent / 100;

}
```

## 🔐 Account and device security

### Account security

1. **Unique, strong passwords** (use a password manager).
2. **Two-step verification** with an **authenticator app**, which is stronger than SMS.
3. A **separate email** for exchange accounts.
4. **Check the web address** carefully, and use **bookmarks**, not search ads or links in messages.
5. Be careful on **public Wi-Fi**.

### Device security

- Keep your phone and computer **updated**.
- Install apps only from **official stores**.
- Do not install unknown **browser extensions** or "booster" apps.
- Never **share your screen** with someone who offers help.

### Sending safely

- **Send a small test** amount first.
- **Copy and paste** addresses, then check the **first and last characters**.
- **Check the network** (many coins exist on several networks): the wrong one can lose funds.
- Transfers are **final**.

> 📌 **Remember:** a wrong address or network usually means your money is gone for good.

::: check Why send a small test first?
+ Mistakes cannot be reversed, so test with a small amount
- It is required by law
- To avoid fees
= Prevent costly errors.
:::

### Scenario: the pop-up

While browsing, a pop-up says: *"Your wallet is at risk! Verify now by entering your 12 words."*

::: check What is it?
+ A phishing attempt. Close it and never enter your recovery phrase
- A real security alert
- A free upgrade
= Real wallets never ask for your phrase on a website.
:::

### If something goes wrong

Move any remaining funds to a **new wallet**, change passwords, tell the **platform and your bank**, keep **evidence**, and report to the **police** and the **regulator**. Do not pay "recovery agents" upfront.

::: check What is the best second factor for exchange logins?
+ An authenticator app
- Sharing the code with support
- No second factor
= Authenticator apps are stronger than SMS.
:::

## 🚨 The most common crypto scams

Crypto scams are everywhere, because transfers are **fast, hard to reverse and easy to hide**.

### The scam catalogue

- **Ponzi and "investment platforms":** promise daily or weekly returns. Early payouts come from new people's money, until it collapses.
- **Romance and "pig butchering" scams:** someone you only know online builds trust over weeks, then pushes you to invest in a fake platform.
- **Impersonation:** fake "support" accounts and fake celebrity giveaways ("send 1, get 2 back").
- **Phishing:** fake websites, emails and messages that steal logins or recovery phrases.
- **Rug pulls:** a new coin is hyped, then its creators take the money and vanish.
- **Fake recovery agents:** they target victims and ask for upfront fees.
- **Fake trading bots and cloud mining** promising guaranteed profit.
- **Fake exchange apps** that show profits but never let you withdraw.

> 📌 **Remember:** **no one gives away free crypto**, and **no one can guarantee profit**.

### The maths of a "5% a day" promise

```playground-js Challenge: try 2 for the daily rate. Is it still believable over a year?
const start = 20000;
const dailyPercent = 5;
const days = 30;

let value = start;
for (let d = 1; d <= days; d++) value = value * (1 + dailyPercent / 100);
console.log(`After ${days} days: ${Math.round(value).toLocaleString()}`);

// And over a year:
const year = start * Math.pow(1 + dailyPercent / 100, 365);
console.log("After 365 days:", year.toExponential(2), "(more money than exists)");
```

::: check Why is "5% a day" impossible to sustain?
+ Compounded daily it would grow beyond all the money in the world
- Because it is too low
- Because banks forbid it
= The maths shows it cannot be real.
:::

### Warning signs, all in one place

1. **Guaranteed or very high returns.**
2. **Urgency** ("join before the price rises").
3. **An online "friend"** guiding your investments.
4. Rewards for **recruiting** others.
5. Deposits are easy, but **withdrawals need extra "fees" or "taxes"**.
6. Requests for your **recovery phrase**.
7. **Unregistered operators** and anonymous founders.

::: check Which request is always a scam?
+ Someone asking for your recovery phrase
- A password reset email you asked for
- A bank statement
= Never share it with anyone.
:::

## ⚖️ Rules, tax and a responsible decision

> ⚠️ **Important:** laws about crypto **change often** and differ by country. This is general awareness, **not legal or tax advice**. Check **current** guidance from official sources, and talk to a qualified professional.

### Regulation

Many countries, including Nigeria, have issued rules about digital assets and the platforms that handle them. Check what the **SEC** and the **central bank** currently say, and prefer **licensed or recognised** platforms.

### Tax and records

Gains or income from crypto may be taxable. **Keep records**: dates, amounts, prices and fees for every purchase, sale and transfer.

::: check Why keep records of crypto transactions?
+ You may need them for tax and to prove where money came from
- They are not important
- Only for friends
= Records protect you.
:::

### Make a responsible decision

Ask yourself:

1. Do I understand what I am buying?
2. Can I afford to lose **all** of it?
3. Do I have an **emergency fund** and **no high-interest debt**?
4. Am I acting from **calm thinking**, or from **FOMO and pressure**?
5. Have I written down my **limit, review dates and exit rules**?

If any answer is **no**, **do not invest yet**. Choosing **not** to buy is a valid, smart decision.

::: check Is choosing not to buy crypto a valid decision?
+ Yes, if it does not fit your goals or risk comfort
- No, everyone must buy
- Only if you are rich
= There is no obligation to take risk.
:::
