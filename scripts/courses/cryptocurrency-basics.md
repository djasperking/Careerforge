## 🧱 What is cryptocurrency, really?

### Digital money with no central owner

Normal money (naira, dollars) is issued by a **central bank** and held by **banks**. Cryptocurrencies run on a **network of computers** with rules written in code. There is no bank in the middle, and usually **no one to call** if you make a mistake.

### Blockchain in plain words

A **blockchain** is a **shared record book** copied across many computers. New transactions are grouped into **blocks**, linked in a chain, and almost impossible to change afterwards.

> 📌 **Remember:** a blockchain is a shared, hard-to-alter record of who owns what.

::: check What is a blockchain?
+ A shared record book copied across many computers
- A bank vault
- A social media app
= Everyone on the network keeps the same record.
:::

### Types of crypto

- **Bitcoin:** the first and best-known cryptocurrency, often compared to "digital gold".
- **Other coins and tokens:** thousands exist, and **most lose value or disappear**.
- **Stablecoins:** designed to track a currency like the dollar. They are **not risk-free**, and some have failed.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** a coin existing on an app does not mean it is valuable or safe.

::: check What is true about thousands of other coins?
+ Many lose value or disappear
- All grow
- All are backed by banks
= Be very careful with unknown coins.
:::

## 👛 Wallets, keys and recovery phrases

### What is a wallet?

A **wallet** does not store coins like a purse. It stores the **keys** that prove you own coins on the blockchain.

- **Public address:** like your account number. Safe to share to receive money.
- **Private key / recovery phrase:** like your PIN and master key combined. **Whoever has it controls your funds.**

> 📌 **Remember:** never share your private key or recovery (seed) phrase. Not with support staff, friends or "recovery services".

::: check Who should know your recovery phrase?
+ Only you, kept safe offline
- Customer support
- A friend
= Anyone who has it can empty your wallet.
:::

### Types of wallets

- **Exchange wallet (custodial):** the exchange holds the keys for you. Easy to use, but you depend on the company.
- **Self-custody wallet (non-custodial):** you hold the keys. Full control, **full responsibility**. Lose the phrase and the money is gone forever.

### Protect your recovery phrase

1. **Write it on paper** (never a screenshot or cloud note).
2. **Store copies in safe, separate places.**
3. **Never type it into a website** unless you are restoring your own wallet on a trusted app.

::: check Where should you store your recovery phrase?
+ Written down offline in a safe place
- In your email
- In a screenshot
= Digital copies can be stolen.
:::

## 🏦 Exchanges and how people buy

An **exchange** is a platform to buy and sell crypto.

### Before you use any exchange

1. **Check regulation:** is it **licensed or recognised** in your country? Rules in Nigeria have changed over time, so check **current** guidance from the **SEC** and the **central bank**.
2. **Check its history:** security record, reviews, how long it has operated.
3. **Check the fees:** trading, withdrawal and exchange-rate spreads.
4. **Check your payment method:** use secure, traceable payments, not strangers' accounts.
5. **Turn on two-step verification** straight away.

> 📌 **Remember:** if a platform fails or is hacked, you might not get your money back.

::: check What should you do before using an exchange?
+ Check if it is regulated, its security record and fees
- Join because friends are
- Send money first and ask later
= Research first.
:::

### Peer-to-peer (P2P) trading

You buy from or sell to another person. **Risks:** fake payment proofs, chargebacks, scam sellers and accounts frozen after suspicious transfers. Use platforms with **escrow**, **never release crypto before the payment has truly arrived in your account**, and ignore screenshots.

::: check In P2P trading, when should you release the crypto?
+ Only after the money has actually arrived in your own account
- After seeing a screenshot
- Before payment
= Screenshots are easy to fake.
:::

## 🎢 Why prices swing so wildly

### Price is driven by belief and supply

Crypto has **no company profits or government backing** behind most coins. Prices move on **news, hype, fear, big holders, and rules**. This leads to **volatility**: fast, large moves up and down.

> **Example:** a coin that rises 50% in a week can fall 50% the next month. If you invest 100,000 and it halves, you hold 50,000. To get back to 100,000 it must **double**.

> 📌 **Remember:** a 50% loss needs a 100% gain to recover.

::: check A holding drops 50%. What gain is needed to get back to the start?
- 50%
+ 100%
- 25%
= Halved money must double.
:::

### Emotion is the enemy

- **FOMO** (fear of missing out) makes people buy at the top.
- **Panic** makes people sell at the bottom.
- **"Everyone is getting rich"** is a classic warning, not a signal.

### Rules for staying sane (if you choose to take part)

1. Only use **money you can afford to lose entirely**.
2. **Never borrow** to buy crypto.
3. Decide **how much** and **when you will review**, in writing, before you start.
4. **Do not check prices all day.**

::: check What does FOMO stand for?
+ Fear of missing out
- Fast order market online
- Free offer money option
= It pushes people to buy at bad times.
:::

## 🚨 The most common crypto scams

Crypto scams are everywhere because transfers are **fast, hard to reverse and anonymous-ish**.

### Common scams

- **Ponzi and "investment platforms":** promise daily or weekly returns (for example 5% a day). Early payouts come from new people's money, until it collapses.
- **Pig butchering / romance scams:** a "friend" you only know online builds trust and pushes you to invest on a fake platform.
- **Impersonation:** fake "support" accounts, fake celebrity giveaways ("send 1 coin, get 2 back").
- **Phishing:** fake websites and links that steal your login or recovery phrase.
- **Rug pulls:** a new coin is hyped, then its creators take the money and vanish.
- **Fake recovery agents:** they target victims, and ask for upfront fees.
- **Fake cloud mining and trading bots** promising guaranteed profit.

> 📌 **Remember:** **no one gives away free crypto**, and **no one can guarantee profit**.

::: check A celebrity "giveaway" says: send 1 coin, get 2 back. What is it?
+ A scam
- A bonus
- A lottery win
= Giveaways like this are fraud.
:::

### Warning signs

1. **Guaranteed or very high returns.**
2. **Urgency and pressure** ("join before the price rises").
3. **Someone you met online** guiding your investments.
4. Rewards for **recruiting** others.
5. A platform that **lets you deposit but delays withdrawals** or asks for extra "fees" or "taxes" first.
6. Requests for your **recovery phrase**.

::: check Which request is always a scam?
+ Someone asking for your recovery phrase
- A normal exchange password reset email you asked for
- A bank statement
= Never share it with anyone.
:::

## 🔐 Keep your crypto and accounts secure

### Account security

1. **Unique, strong passwords** (use a password manager).
2. **Two-step verification** with an **authenticator app** (better than SMS).
3. **Separate email** for your exchange accounts.
4. **Check the web address** carefully, and use **bookmarks** instead of search ads or links in messages.
5. Be careful with **public Wi-Fi**.

### Device security

- Keep your phone and computer **updated**.
- Install apps only from **official stores**.
- Do not install **unknown apps** or **browser extensions** to "boost" crypto.
- Never **screenshare** with someone who offers help.

### Sending safely

- **Send a small test** amount first.
- **Copy and paste** addresses, then check the **first and last characters**.
- **Check the network** (many coins exist on several networks), because the wrong one can lose funds.
- Crypto transfers are **final**, as there is no reversal.

> 📌 **Remember:** a wrong address or network means your money is probably gone for good.

::: check Why send a small test first?
+ Mistakes cannot be reversed, so test with a small amount
- It is required by law
- To avoid fees
= Prevent costly errors.
:::

### If something goes wrong

Move any remaining funds to a **new wallet**, change passwords, tell the **platform and your bank**, keep **evidence**, and report to the **police** and the **regulator**. Do not pay "recovery agents" upfront.

::: check What is the best second factor for exchange logins?
+ An authenticator app
- Sharing the code with support
- No second factor
= Authenticator apps are stronger than SMS.
:::

## ⚖️ Rules, tax and a responsible decision

> ⚠️ **Important:** laws about crypto **change often** and differ by country. This is general awareness, **not legal or tax advice**. Check **current** guidance from official sources, and talk to a qualified professional.

### Regulation

Many countries, including Nigeria, have issued rules about digital assets and the platforms that handle them. Check what the **SEC** and the **central bank** currently say, and use **licensed or recognised** platforms.

### Tax and records

Gains or income from crypto may be taxable. **Keep records**: dates, amounts, prices and fees for every purchase, sale and transfer.

::: check Why keep records of crypto transactions?
+ You may need them for tax and to prove where money came from
- They are not important
- Only for friends
= Records protect you.
:::

### Make a responsible decision

Ask yourself:

1. Do I understand what I am buying?
2. Can I afford to lose **all** of it?
3. Do I have an **emergency fund** and **no high-interest debt**?
4. Am I acting from **calm thinking**, or **FOMO or pressure**?
5. Have I written down my **limit and my exit rules**?

If you answer "no" to any, **do not invest yet**. Choosing **not** to buy is a valid, smart decision.

::: check Is choosing not to invest in crypto a valid decision?
+ Yes, if it does not fit your goals or risk comfort
- No, everyone must buy
- Only if you are rich
= There is no obligation to take risk.
:::
