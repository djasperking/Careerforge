## 👋 Welcome: become a virtual assistant

Business owners, coaches and busy professionals everywhere need help with the tasks that eat their day: emails, scheduling, research, data entry, social media. A **virtual assistant (VA)** does that work **remotely**, so they can focus on what only they can do.

### What you will do in this course

- 🗂️ Learn what a VA does and which services to offer
- 🏷️ Practise **scored drills** on inbox triage, time zones, priorities and safety
- 📧 Write real professional emails
- 📅 Schedule meetings across time zones
- ✅ Build a task board and a research report
- 🧪 Finish with a practical **VA Starter Project**

> 📌 **Remember:** a VA saves other people time. Reliability is your biggest selling point.

::: check What is the main job of a virtual assistant?
- Run the client's whole business alone
+ Take over time-consuming tasks so the client can focus
- Only answer phone calls
= VAs support clients with tasks, often remotely.
:::

### An honest note

Success as a VA comes from **skills, reliability and finding clients**, not luck. Income varies by experience, services and market, and **no income is guaranteed**. Never pay anyone for a "guaranteed VA job".

::: check Should you pay a fee to be handed a guaranteed VA job?
- Yes, it secures your place
+ No. That is a common scam
- Only a small one
= Real clients pay you; you never pay them to hire you.
:::

## 🧰 Services you can offer

You do not have to do everything. Pick **two or three** services and be great at them.

### Common VA services

- **Admin support:** email, calendar, data entry, document formatting.
- **Customer support:** answering messages and tickets.
- **Social media support:** scheduling posts, replying to comments.
- **Research:** finding leads, comparing options, summarising findings.
- **Bookkeeping basics:** tracking invoices and expenses (with training).
- **Content help:** proofreading, simple graphics, transcription.

> 📌 **Remember:** a clear niche, like "admin support for coaches", is easier to sell than "I can do anything".

::: check Why is a clear service niche useful?
+ Clients can quickly see what you are good at
- It lets you charge nothing
- It means you can only have one client
= Specific is easier to trust and to hire.
:::

### Match services to your strengths

Ask yourself:

1. What do I enjoy and do well? (writing, organising, numbers, design)
2. What tools do I already know? (phone apps, Google tools, Canva)
3. Who could I help? (small businesses, coaches, authors, shops)

::: check Which question helps you pick services?
- What is the cheapest thing I can offer?
+ What am I already good at, and who needs it?
- What does nobody else want?
= Start with your strengths and real client needs.
:::

## 📧 Drill 1: inbox triage

For many clients, an organised inbox is worth gold.

### Four moves

1. **Delete or archive:** needs nothing.
2. **Reply now:** takes under 2 minutes and you can answer it.
3. **Schedule:** needs time or a decision. Turn it into a task or calendar slot.
4. **Delegate or forward:** belongs to someone else.

> 📌 **Remember:** triage means **decide once** and move on.

::: check A client email can be answered in one minute. What do you do?
- Leave it for later
+ Reply now
- Delete it
= Quick replies keep the inbox moving.
:::

### Labels

Use simple labels such as **Urgent**, **Waiting for reply**, **Invoices** and **Read later**. Fewer labels beat many confusing ones.

### The drill

::: drill Inbox triage
labels: Reply now | Schedule | Delegate | Delete or archive
rule: Reply now if it takes under 2 minutes and you can answer it. Schedule if it needs time or a decision from the client. Delegate if it belongs to someone else. Delete or archive if it needs nothing.
pass: 75
- A newsletter from a shop the client never uses. => Delete or archive | It needs nothing.
- A customer asks: What are your opening hours? => Reply now | A quick, known answer.
- An invoice for 45,000 naira due on Friday. => Schedule | Turn it into a task with a due date.
- A message that belongs to the client's accountant. => Delegate | Forward it to the right person.
- An invitation to speak at an event next month. => Schedule | It needs the client's decision.
- A supplier asks you to confirm a delivery date you already have. => Reply now | A quick, known answer.
- A promotional email about a software discount. => Delete or archive | It needs nothing.
- A question about the client's tax return. => Delegate | It belongs to the accountant.
:::

### Writing a professional email

A good email has:

- A **clear subject:** "Meeting request: Tuesday 2pm" (not "Hi").
- A **short greeting** and the **point in the first line**.
- **One request** per paragraph and clear next steps.
- A polite **sign-off** with your name.

> **Example:**
> Subject: Quote request for 50 flyers
> Hello Mr Bello, could you send a quote for 50 flyers (A5, colour) by Friday? Thank you. Best regards, Ada

## ✉️ Drill 2: subject lines and tone

A subject line decides whether an email is opened and understood.

### What a good subject line does

- **Names the topic** ("Invoice 0042").
- **States the action** ("Please confirm by Friday").
- **Avoids** ALL CAPS and "URGENT!!!" unless it truly is.

### The drill

::: drill Better subject line
labels: Option A | Option B
rule: Choose the subject line that is specific, names the topic and, where needed, the action or deadline.
pass: 80
- A: Hi. B: Meeting request: Tuesday 2pm (Lagos time) => Option B | It names the topic and time.
- A: Invoice 0042 for September, due 30 Sept. B: Invoice => Option A | It is specific and has a date.
- A: URGENT!!! PLEASE READ. B: Quote needed for 100 T-shirts by Friday => Option B | It is specific and calm.
- A: Question. B: Question about the delivery date for order 118 => Option B | It names the topic.
- A: Following up: proposal sent on 5 October. B: ??? => Option A | It is clear and polite.
:::

### Reply templates

Save templates for things you write often: "Thanks, received", "Here are the available times", "Following up". They save minutes every day, but **personalise** each one.

> 💡 **Tip:** re-read before sending. Check names, dates, attachments and tone.

::: check Why save reply templates?
+ They save time and keep your messages consistent
- They replace thinking
- They are required by email apps
= Reuse what you write often, and adjust it each time.
:::

## 🌍 Drill 3: calendars and time zones

### A good calendar

- Put **everything** in the calendar: meetings, deadlines, focus time.
- Add **links and details** (video link, phone number, agenda).
- Set **reminders** (for example 1 day and 15 minutes before).
- Leave **buffer time** between meetings.

> 📌 **Remember:** if it is not in the calendar, it did not happen.

### Scheduling a meeting step by step

1. Check the client's availability.
2. Offer **two or three options**.
3. Confirm with the **date, time and time zone**.
4. Send the invite with the meeting link.
5. Send a reminder the day before.

### Time zones: the rule of thumb

Every place is a number of hours ahead of or behind **UTC**. To convert, move the hours by the **difference** between the two places.

For this drill, assume it is **winter** and use these offsets:

| Place | Offset from UTC |
|---|---|
| Lagos (WAT) | +1 |
| London (GMT) | 0 |
| New York (EST) | -5 |
| Nairobi (EAT) | +3 |
| Dubai (GST) | +4 |

> **Example:** 3pm in London (0) is **4pm in Lagos** (+1), because Lagos is one hour ahead.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** many places change their clocks in summer, and the dates differ. **Always check a time zone converter** before you confirm, and always write the **time zone** in the invite.

::: check Lagos is +1 and Dubai is +4. When it is 6pm in Dubai, what time is it in Lagos?
- 6pm
+ 3pm
- 9pm
= Dubai is 3 hours ahead of Lagos, so subtract 3 hours.
:::

### The drill

::: drill Convert the time
labels: 1pm | 3pm | 4am | 4pm | 5pm
rule: Use winter offsets: Lagos +1, London 0, New York -5, Nairobi +3, Dubai +4. Move the time by the difference between the two places.
pass: 70
- A call is at 3pm in London. What time is it in Lagos? => 4pm | Lagos is 1 hour ahead of London.
- A meeting is at 10am in Lagos. What time is it in New York? => 4am | New York is 6 hours behind Lagos.
- A call is at 2pm in Lagos. What time is it in London? => 1pm | London is 1 hour behind Lagos.
- A client in Nairobi suggests 5pm Nairobi time. What time is it in Lagos? => 3pm | Nairobi is 2 hours ahead of Lagos.
- A webinar starts at 9am in New York. What time is it in Lagos? => 3pm | Lagos is 6 hours ahead of New York.
- A partner in Dubai suggests 6pm Dubai time. What time is it in Lagos? => 3pm | Dubai is 3 hours ahead of Lagos.
:::

## ✅ Drill 4: tasks and priorities

### A simple task system

Use a free tool (Google Tasks, Trello or a simple sheet). Keep **three columns**:

- **To do**
- **Doing**
- **Done**

Give each task an **owner**, a **due date** and a **priority**.

> 📌 **Remember:** every task needs an owner and a due date, or it will not get done.

::: check What two things should every task have?
+ An owner and a due date
- A colour and an emoji
- A long description
= Clear ownership and timing keeps work moving.
:::

### Prioritise with urgent versus important

- **Do now:** urgent and important.
- **Schedule:** important, not urgent.
- **Delegate:** not your job, or not important enough for the client's time.
- **Drop:** neither.

### The drill

::: drill Prioritise
labels: Do now | Schedule | Delegate | Drop
rule: Do now if it is urgent and important. Schedule if it is important but not urgent. Delegate if it belongs to someone else. Drop if it is neither urgent nor important.
pass: 75
- The client's payment is two weeks overdue. => Do now | It is urgent and affects money.
- Plan next quarter's content calendar. => Schedule | Important, but not urgent.
- A stranger on LinkedIn is selling a course. => Drop | Neither urgent nor important.
- File the client's tax returns (not your skill). => Delegate | It belongs to the accountant.
- The client's flight check-in opens in two hours. => Do now | Urgent and important.
- Research CRM tools for next month. => Schedule | Important, not urgent.
- Reply to a spam survey. => Drop | It does not matter.
- The client's website is down and customers are complaining. => Do now | Urgent and important.
:::

### Reporting back

Clients love short updates: **"Done: 12 emails sorted. Waiting: reply from supplier. Next: draft invoice."** Send one on a regular day.

> 💡 **Tip:** if you will miss a deadline, tell the client early and give a new time. Silence is worse than delay.

::: check If you will miss a deadline, the best thing to do is...
- Stay silent
+ Tell the client early and give a new time
- Blame the client
= Honest, early communication builds trust.
:::

## 🔍 Research and clear reporting

### A simple research method

1. **Understand the question.** What exactly does the client need to decide?
2. **Search** with clear words, and use more than one source.
3. **Check** that sources are recent and trustworthy.
4. **Summarise** in a short, useful answer.

> 📌 **Remember:** the client wants an answer, not 40 links.

### Check your sources

- Is the page **recent**?
- Who wrote it? A real company or expert?
- Do **two sources agree**?
- Is it **selling** something?

### Presenting findings

Use a small table, then add a **one-line recommendation**.

| Tool | Price per month | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tool A | 4,000 | Simple, quick setup | Few features |
| Tool B | 8,000 | Many features, reports | Harder to learn |
| Tool C | 0 (free plan) | Free, enough for one person | Limited storage |

> **Recommendation:** "For a one-person shop, I suggest Tool C to start, and Tool A if you need more storage."

::: check What should end a research report?
- Nothing
+ A clear recommendation
- More questions
= Make it easy for the client to decide.
:::

### Good habits

- Note the **date and source** for every key fact.
- Say **"I could not verify this"** instead of guessing.
- Keep the report to **one page** unless asked for more.

::: check What do you do when you cannot confirm a fact?
+ Say you could not verify it
- Guess
- Leave it out silently
= Honesty protects your credibility.
:::

## 🤝 Communicating and setting expectations

### Start clear

Before you begin, agree in writing:

- **Scope:** exactly what you will do.
- **Hours or deliverables:** how much work.
- **Price and payment:** how much, when and how.
- **Communication:** how and how often you will talk.
- **Availability:** your working hours and response time.

> 📌 **Remember:** write the agreement down. Clear scope prevents most disputes.

::: check Why write the agreement down?
+ It prevents misunderstandings about scope and payment
- It is only for large companies
- It is not needed for small jobs
= Even a short message saying what was agreed protects both sides.
:::

### A simple agreement message

> Hello Chioma, to confirm: I will manage your inbox and calendar for 10 hours a week (Mon to Fri, 9am to 1pm WAT), at 3,000 naira an hour, invoiced every Friday and paid within 3 days. I will reply to messages within 4 hours on working days. I will send a short report each Friday. Please reply "agreed" to confirm.

### Professional communication

- Reply within a clear window.
- Keep messages short and polite.
- **Confirm tasks** back: "Got it: I will send the report by Thursday."
- Ask questions early, not after you are stuck.

### Handling problems

If a client is unhappy or you make a mistake: **own it, fix it, and prevent it**. "Sorry, I sent the wrong file. Here is the correct one, and I now double-check attachments."

::: check What is the best response to your own mistake?
- Hide it
+ Own it, fix it, and say how you will prevent it
- Blame the client
= Clients respect honesty and quick fixes.
:::

## 🔒 Drill 5: confidentiality and safety

VAs often see passwords, money and private messages. **Never share or use it outside the task.**

### The rules

- Receive passwords through a **password manager** or the client's secure method, never in a public chat.
- Use **strong, different passwords** and **two-step verification**.
- **Ask first** before sharing any client information.
- Do not store client files on **shared or public computers**.
- **Log out** when you finish on any shared device.

> 📌 **Remember:** trust is your most valuable asset. Protect client information carefully.

### The drill

::: drill Safe or unsafe
labels: Safe | Unsafe
rule: Safe practices protect the client's information and accounts. Unsafe practices expose it, share it without permission, or make it easy to steal.
pass: 75
- Receive the client's password through a password manager share. => Safe | It is a secure method.
- Paste the client's password into a group chat. => Unsafe | Others can see it.
- Turn on two-step verification for client accounts. => Safe | It protects against theft.
- Save client files on a shared cafe computer. => Unsafe | Others may access them.
- Use the same password for yourself and the client's accounts. => Unsafe | One leak exposes both.
- Ask the client before sharing any of their information. => Safe | It respects their privacy.
- Send a client's bank details to a friend to ask for advice. => Unsafe | It shares private data.
- Log out of client accounts when finished on a shared device. => Safe | It prevents misuse.
:::

### Boundaries

- Work in your **agreed hours**, and say no politely to unpaid extra work.
- Do not work on illegal or dishonest tasks.
- Keep your own accounts separate from the client's.

::: check A new "client" sends you a cheque and asks you to send part of it back. What is this?

- A normal payment
- A bonus
= Overpayment scams rely on fake cheques and urgency.

## 💼 Drill 6: pricing and spotting bad clients

### Ways to price

- **Hourly:** simple for new VAs. A fair rate reflects your skills and your market.
- **Package:** "10 hours of admin support per month."
- **Project:** a fixed price for a clear job, like "set up a calendar system".

> 📌 **Remember:** start with a fair, clear price and raise it as you gain experience and results.

::: check Which pricing method is a fixed price for a clear job?
- Hourly
+ Project
- Free
= Project pricing is one price for defined work.
:::

### Do the maths

> **Example:** 12 hours a week x 3,000 naira = 36,000 a week, about 144,000 a month. Subtract your costs (data, tools), and remember not every hour can be billed.

### Client red flags

Some clients are risky. Learn the signs.

::: drill Safe client or red flag
labels: Safe client | Red flag
rule: A safe client agrees scope and price clearly and never asks you to pay or share secrets. A red flag asks for money, rushes you, or avoids any agreement.
pass: 75
- Sends a cheque and asks you to return part of it. => Red flag | Overpayment scam.
- Asks for a clear written scope and agrees a price. => Safe client | Clear and professional.
- Wants you to pay a deposit to get the job. => Red flag | You should never pay to work.
- Pays a deposit and signs a short agreement. => Safe client | Money moves toward you, with a written agreement.
- Refuses any agreement and rushes you to start. => Red flag | No agreement and pressure.
- Asks you to log into their personal bank account using their PIN. => Red flag | Never use a client's PIN.
- Gives you access through a business tool with your own login. => Safe client | Proper, traceable access.
:::

### Where to find clients

1. **People you know:** friends, family and local business owners.
2. **Social media:** share tips and say you offer VA services.
3. **Freelance platforms** and **job boards**, including the Career Forge Jobs page.
4. **Direct outreach:** a short, personal message to businesses that need help.

> **Outreach example:** "Hello Ada, I noticed your shop posts on Instagram but replies take a while. I help small businesses manage messages. Could I do a free one-hour trial so you can see how it works? Thanks, Chioma"

::: check What makes an outreach message stronger?
- It says "I need a job"
+ It names a specific problem you can solve
- It is very long
= Offer value first, not a request for charity.
:::
