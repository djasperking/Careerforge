## 👋 Welcome: become a virtual assistant

Business owners, coaches and busy professionals everywhere need help with the tasks that eat their day: emails, scheduling, research, data entry, social media. A **virtual assistant (VA)** does that work **remotely**, so they can focus on what only they can do.

### What you will learn

- 📧 Email and inbox management
- 📅 Calendars, scheduling and time zones
- ✅ Task systems and reminders
- 🔍 Research and clear reporting
- 🤝 Working well with clients
- 💼 Building a profile and sample work

> 📌 **Remember:** a VA saves other people time. Reliability is your biggest selling point.

Success as a VA comes from **skills, reliability and finding clients**, not luck. Income varies by experience, services and market. Never pay anyone for a "guaranteed VA job".

## 🧰 Services you can offer

You do not have to do everything. Pick **two or three** services and be great at them.

### Common VA services

- **Admin support:** email, calendar, data entry, document formatting.
- **Customer support:** answering messages and tickets.
- **Social media support:** scheduling posts, replying to comments.
- **Research:** finding leads, comparing options, summarising findings.
- **Bookkeeping basics:** tracking invoices and expenses (with training).
- **Content help:** proofreading, simple graphics, transcription.

> 📌 **Remember:** a clear niche, like "admin support for coaches", is easier to sell than "I can do anything".

::: check Why is a clear service niche useful?
+ Clients can quickly see what you are good at
- It lets you charge nothing
- It means you can only have one client
= Specific is easier to trust and to hire.
:::

### Match services to your strengths

Ask yourself:

1. What do I enjoy and do well? (writing, organising, numbers, design)
2. What tools do I already know? (phone apps, Google tools, Canva)
3. Who could I help? (small businesses, coaches, authors, shops)

::: check Which question helps you pick services?
- What is the cheapest thing I can offer?
+ What am I already good at, and who needs it?
- What does nobody else want?
= Start with your strengths and real client needs.
:::

## 📧 Email and inbox management

For many clients, an organised inbox is worth gold.

### Triage: sort fast

Go through email with four actions:

1. **Delete or archive** what needs nothing.
2. **Reply now** if it takes under 2 minutes.
3. **Schedule** it (turn it into a task or calendar slot).
4. **Forward or delegate** to the right person.

> 📌 **Remember:** triage means decide once: delete, reply, schedule or delegate.

::: check A client email can be answered in one minute. What do you do?
- Leave it for later
+ Reply now
- Delete it
= Quick replies keep the inbox moving.
:::

### Labels and folders

Use simple labels such as **Urgent**, **Waiting for reply**, **Invoices** and **Read later**. Fewer labels beat many confusing ones.

### Writing a professional email

A good email has:

- A **clear subject:** "Meeting request: Tuesday 2pm" (not "Hi").
- A **short greeting** and the **point in the first line**.
- **One request** per paragraph, and clear next steps.
- A polite **sign-off** with your name.

> **Example:**
> Subject: Quote request for 50 flyers
> Hello Mr Bello, could you send a quote for 50 flyers (A5, colour) by Friday? Thank you. Best regards, Ada

::: check Which subject line is best?
- Hi
- Question!!!
+ Quote request for 50 flyers
= A specific subject helps people act quickly.
:::

### Reply templates

Save templates for things you write often: "Thanks, received," "Here are the available times," "Following up." They save minutes every day.

> 💡 **Tip:** always re-read an email before sending. Check names, dates, attachments and tone.

::: check Why save reply templates?
+ They save time and keep your messages consistent
- They replace thinking
- They are required by email apps
= Reuse what you write often, and adjust it each time.
:::

## 📅 Calendars, scheduling and time zones

### A good calendar

- Put **everything** in the calendar: meetings, deadlines, focus time.
- Add **links and details** (video link, phone number, agenda).
- Set **reminders** (for example 1 day and 15 minutes before).
- Leave **buffer time** between meetings.

> 📌 **Remember:** if it is not in the calendar, it did not happen.

::: check Why add buffer time between meetings?
+ People need breaks and meetings often run over
- Calendars require it
- To make the day look busier
= Buffers prevent stress and double-booking.
:::

### Scheduling a meeting step by step

1. Check the client's availability.
2. Offer **2 or 3 time options** to the other person.
3. Confirm with the date, time **and time zone**.
4. Send the invite with the meeting link.
5. Send a reminder the day before.

### Time zones

Clients can be in other countries. Always write the **time zone**: "Tuesday 2pm WAT (Lagos) / 1pm GMT".

- Nigeria is on **WAT** (UTC+1) all year; many places change their clocks, so check dates.
- Use a free time zone converter before you confirm.

::: check What must you include when scheduling with someone abroad?
- Only the time
+ The date, time and time zone
- Nothing, they will guess
= Time zone mistakes cause missed meetings.
:::

### Practice

**A client in London (GMT) asks for a call at 3pm London time. London is 1 hour behind Lagos (WAT) at that time of year.** What time is it in Lagos?

Answer: 4pm WAT.

::: check London is 1 hour behind Lagos. A call at 3pm London is what time in Lagos?
- 2pm
- 3pm
+ 4pm
= Lagos is ahead, so add an hour.
:::

## ✅ Tasks, reminders and organisation

### A simple task system

Use a free tool (Google Tasks, Trello or a simple sheet). Keep **three columns**:

- **To do**
- **Doing**
- **Done**

Give each task an **owner**, a **due date** and a **priority**.

> 📌 **Remember:** every task needs an owner and a due date, or it will not get done.

::: check What two things should every task have?
+ An owner and a due date
- A colour and an emoji
- A long description
= Clear ownership and timing keeps work moving.
:::

### Prioritise with Urgent vs Important

- **Urgent and important:** do it now.
- **Important, not urgent:** schedule it.
- **Urgent, not important:** delegate or do it quickly.
- **Neither:** drop it.

::: check A task is important but not urgent. What do you do?
+ Schedule it
- Ignore it
- Do it before everything
= Plan important work before it becomes an emergency.
:::

### Reporting back

Clients love short updates: **"Done: 12 emails sorted. Waiting: reply from supplier. Next: draft invoice."** Send one on a regular day, like Friday.

> 💡 **Tip:** if you will miss a deadline, tell the client early and give a new time. Silence is worse than delay.

::: check If you will miss a deadline, the best thing to do is...
- Stay silent
+ Tell the client early and give a new time
- Blame the client
= Honest, early communication builds trust.
:::

## 🔍 Research and clear reporting

### A simple research method

1. **Understand the question.** What exactly does the client need to decide?
2. **Search** with clear words, and use more than one source.
3. **Check** that sources are recent and trustworthy.
4. **Summarise** in a short, useful answer.

> 📌 **Remember:** the client wants an answer, not 40 links.

::: check What does a client want from research?
- A huge list of links
+ A short, useful answer with sources
- Your personal opinion only
= Summarise the findings so they can decide quickly.
:::

### Check your sources

- Is the page **recent**?
- Who wrote it? Is it a real company or an expert?
- Do **two sources** agree?

### Presenting findings

Use a simple table:

| Option | Price | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tool A | 5,000/month | Easy to use | Few features |
| Tool B | 9,000/month | Many features | Harder to learn |

Then add a **one-line recommendation**: "I suggest Tool A because it fits your budget."

::: check What should end a research report?
- Nothing
+ A clear recommendation
- More questions
= Make it easy for the client to decide.
:::

## 🤝 Communicating and setting expectations

### Start clear

Before you begin, agree in writing:

- **Scope:** exactly what you will do.
- **Hours or deliverables:** how much work.
- **Price and payment:** how much, when and how.
- **Communication:** how and how often you will talk.
- **Availability:** your working hours and response time.

> 📌 **Remember:** write the agreement down. Clear scope prevents most disputes.

::: check Why write the agreement down?
+ It prevents misunderstandings about scope and payment
- It is only for large companies
- It is not needed for small jobs
= Even a short message saying what was agreed protects both sides.
:::

### Professional communication

- Reply within a clear window (for example, within 24 hours on workdays).
- Keep messages short and polite.
- **Confirm tasks** back: "Got it: I will send the report by Thursday."
- Ask questions early, not after you are stuck.

### Handling problems

If a client is unhappy or you make a mistake: **own it, fix it, and prevent it**. "Sorry, I sent the wrong file. Here is the correct one, and I now double-check attachments."

::: check What is the best response to your own mistake?
- Hide it
+ Own it, fix it, and say how you will prevent it
- Blame the client
= Clients respect honesty and quick fixes.
:::

## 🔒 Confidentiality, boundaries and safety

### Keep client information private

VAs often see passwords, money and private messages. **Never share or use it outside the task.**

- Use a **password manager** or the client's secure sharing. Do not paste passwords in chat.
- Use **strong passwords** and two-step verification.
- Do not store client files on shared computers.

> 📌 **Remember:** trust is your most valuable asset. Protect client information carefully.

::: check How should you receive a client's password?
- In a public group chat
+ Through a password manager or the client's secure method
- On a sticky note
= Secure sharing keeps accounts safe.
:::

### Boundaries

- Work in your **agreed hours**, and say no to unpaid extra work politely.
- Do not work on illegal or dishonest tasks.
- Keep your own accounts separate from the client's.

### Staying safe from scams

- Never accept payment before you do the work **if it asks you to send money back**.
- Be careful with clients who refuse a clear contract or want everything off-platform.
- Never share your personal banking PIN or OTP.

::: check A new "client" sends you a cheque and asks you to send part of it back. What is this?
+ A classic scam
- A normal payment
- A bonus
= Overpayment scams rely on fake cheques and urgency.
:::

## 💼 Pricing and finding your first client

### Ways to price

- **Hourly:** simple for new VAs. A fair rate reflects your skills and your market.
- **Package:** "10 hours of admin support per month."
- **Project:** a fixed price for a clear job, like "set up a calendar system".

> 📌 **Remember:** start with a fair, clear price and raise it as you gain experience and results.

::: check Which pricing method is a fixed price for a clear job?
- Hourly
+ Project
- Free
= Project pricing is one price for defined work.
:::

### Where to find clients

1. **People you know:** friends, family and local business owners.
2. **Social media:** share useful tips and say you offer VA services.
3. **Freelance platforms** and **job boards**, such as the Career Forge Jobs page.
4. **Direct outreach:** a short, personal message to businesses that need help.

### A short outreach message

> Hello Ada, I noticed your shop posts on Instagram but replies take a while. I help small businesses manage inboxes and messages. Could I do a free one-hour trial so you can see how it works? Thanks, Chioma

> 💡 **Tip:** be specific about the problem you can solve. Generic "I need work" messages are ignored.

::: check What makes an outreach message stronger?
- It says "I need a job"
+ It names a specific problem you can solve
- It is very long
= Offer value first, not a request for charity.
:::
