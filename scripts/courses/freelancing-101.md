## 👋 Welcome: what freelancing really is

A **freelancer** sells a skill directly to clients instead of working for one employer. You choose your clients, set your prices and work from anywhere. You also handle finding work, deadlines and getting paid.

### What you will learn

- 🎯 Choosing a service and a target client
- 🧱 Building a profile and portfolio
- 🔎 Finding clients
- ✍️ Writing proposals that win
- 💰 Pricing and getting paid safely
- 🤝 Keeping clients

> 📌 **Remember:** freelancing is a business. Your skill is the product, and you are the salesperson too.

::: check What does a freelancer do?
- Works for one employer only
+ Sells a skill directly to clients
- Never works with clients
= Freelancers are self-employed service providers.
:::

### An honest note

Early freelancing can be slow. It often takes **weeks of outreach** to land the first client, and income can be uneven. Anyone promising quick guaranteed money is selling a dream, so be careful.

::: check What should you expect early in freelancing?
- Instant guaranteed income
+ Effort and patience before the first client
- Clients finding you without trying
= Consistent outreach is how most freelancers start.
:::

## 🎯 Choose your service and your client

### Skill + client + result

A strong offer finishes this sentence: **"I help [type of client] to [get a result] by [what I do]."**

- "I help small restaurants get more orders by managing their Instagram."
- "I help students improve their grades by tutoring maths online."
- "I help shops save time by organising their inventory spreadsheets."

> 📌 **Remember:** a specific offer is easier to sell than "I can do anything".

::: check Which offer is strongest?
- I do everything
+ I help small shops save time by organising their stock spreadsheets
- I need money
= It names the client, the result and the service.
:::

### Pick the sweet spot

Look for the overlap of:

1. What you can **do well**.
2. What people **pay for**.
3. What you **enjoy** enough to repeat.

### Beginner-friendly services

Writing and proofreading, graphic design, virtual assistance, social media help, data entry and spreadsheets, transcription, tutoring, simple websites, customer support.

::: check Which three things make a good service choice?
+ Skill, demand and something you enjoy
- Only what is easiest
- Only what pays the most
= You need all three to stay motivated and earn.
:::

## 🧱 Profile and portfolio

### A profile that sells

- **Headline:** the result you deliver, such as "Logo and social media designer for small businesses".
- **Photo:** clear and friendly.
- **Bio:** 3 to 4 sentences: who you help, how, and your proof.
- **Services:** 2 or 3 clearly listed.
- **Contact:** email and a link to your work.

> 📌 **Remember:** clients decide in seconds. A clear headline and proof make you look reliable.

::: check What should your headline say?
+ The result you deliver and for whom
- Your age
- Just "freelancer"
= Clients search for outcomes.
:::

### Portfolio: proof you can do it

No clients yet? Make **sample projects**:

- Redesign a local shop's flyer.
- Write a sample blog post or product description.
- Build a sample budget sheet or content calendar.

Pick **3 to 5 best pieces**. Add **one line** for each: the problem and what you did.

::: check How can a beginner build a portfolio without clients?
- They cannot
+ By making sample projects that show their skill
- By copying other people's work
= Never copy. Create your own samples.
:::

### Testimonials

After every project, ask the client for **one or two sentences** of feedback. Real testimonials build trust quickly. Start with friends or small free trials, and mention it honestly.

## 🔎 Where to find clients

### Four sources

1. **Your own circle:** friends, family, church, school and local businesses.
2. **Social media:** show your work, share tips, and make your offer clear.
3. **Freelance platforms and job boards:** create a profile and apply to suitable posts, including the Career Forge **Jobs** page.
4. **Direct outreach:** write personally to businesses that need your service.

> 📌 **Remember:** most beginners get their first clients from people they know or direct outreach, not by waiting.

::: check Where do many beginners find their first clients?
+ People they know and direct outreach
- Waiting for strangers to find them
- Paying for a job
= Start close to home and reach out.
:::

### Direct outreach that works

1. **Find** businesses with a clear problem (a shop with few posts, no website, messy records).
2. **Say** one specific thing you noticed.
3. **Offer** a small, easy next step.

> **Example:** "Hello Ada, I noticed your bakery posts great cakes but gets few comments. I help small businesses grow on Instagram. Can I send you 3 free post ideas?"

### Numbers game

Plan to contact **5 businesses a day** for a few weeks and track replies in a sheet. Most will not answer, which is normal.

::: check What makes outreach stronger?
- A generic message to everyone
+ A specific observation and a small offer
- Begging for work
= Show you looked at their business.
:::

### Platform safety

- Keep **messages and payments inside the platform** when it offers protection.
- Be careful of clients who rush you, refuse a contract or ask you to pay first.

## ✍️ Proposals that win

### What a proposal is

A proposal is a **short message** telling a client how you will solve **their** problem. Most freelancers lose work because they write about themselves instead of the client.

### The simple structure

1. **Hook:** show you read their post (one sentence about their need).
2. **Solution:** how you will do it, in 2 to 3 steps.
3. **Proof:** one relevant sample or result.
4. **Details:** price, timeline.
5. **Next step:** a clear, easy question.

> 📌 **Remember:** talk about the client's problem first, and about yourself second.

::: check What should a proposal start with?
- A long story about your life
+ A line showing you understand the client's need
- Your price
= Lead with their problem.
:::

### Example

> Hello Mr Bello, I read that you need 10 product descriptions for your online shoe store. I will write clear, friendly descriptions of about 80 words each, with the keywords you want, and send them within 3 days. Here is a sample I wrote for a bag shop: [link]. The price is 15,000 naira for all 10. Would you like me to start with 2 as a trial?

### Mistakes to avoid

- Copying one message to everyone.
- Spelling mistakes.
- Being vague about price or time.
- Writing five paragraphs about yourself.

::: check Which is a proposal mistake?
+ Sending the same generic message to every client
- Mentioning the client's need
- Giving a clear price
= Tailor every proposal.
:::

## 💰 Pricing and getting paid safely

### Ways to price

- **Hourly:** easy to start, but rewards slowness.
- **Per project:** one price for a clear result. Clients like certainty.
- **Retainer:** a monthly fee for ongoing work.

### Setting a fair price

1. Estimate **how many hours** the job really takes (and add a buffer).
2. Decide the **hourly rate** you want to earn.
3. Add costs (data, tools, transaction fees).
4. Check what **others charge** in your market.

> 📌 **Remember:** price for the result and the time, not for how nervous you feel. Do not undervalue yourself, and do not overcharge without proof.

::: check Why add a time buffer when estimating?
+ Jobs usually take longer than expected
- It makes the price look bigger
- Clients like waiting
= A buffer protects you from underpaid work.
:::

### Getting paid safely

- **Agree the price and scope in writing** before starting.
- For new clients, ask for a **deposit** (for example 30 to 50%) or work in **small milestones**.
- Use **trusted payment methods** with proof of payment.
- **Never** hand over final files before payment on a new client when you are working directly.
- Beware of **overpayment scams**: a "client" sends too much and asks you to send the difference back.

::: check A new client sends too much money and asks you to refund the extra. What is this?
+ A common overpayment scam
- A bonus
- Normal
= Do not refund until the money has truly cleared and is verified.
:::

### Invoices and records

Send a simple invoice with your name, the client's name, the work, the amount and the due date. Keep records of income for tax.

## 🤝 Delivering and keeping clients

### Agree scope clearly

Write what is **included** (for example 10 descriptions, 2 revisions) and what **costs extra**. Most disputes come from fuzzy scope.

> 📌 **Remember:** "2 revisions included" saves you from endless changes.

::: check Why state the number of revisions?
+ It stops endless changes and protects your time
- It annoys clients
- It is not allowed
= Clear limits are professional.
:::

### Deliver like a pro

- **Update early:** a quick message when you start and halfway.
- **Deliver on time**, or tell the client early if you need more.
- **Check your work** twice. Fix small errors before sending.
- **Make it easy:** organised files with clear names.

### Handling feedback

Listen, ask what they want changed, and fix it politely. If it is outside the agreed scope, say so kindly and offer a price.

### Turn clients into repeat business

1. Ask for a **testimonial** and a **referral**.
2. Suggest a **next step** ("Shall I also set up your next 4 posts?").
3. Stay in touch every month or two.

::: check What is a good way to get more work from a happy client?
- Ghost them
+ Ask for a testimonial and suggest a next step
- Raise your price suddenly
= Happy clients are your best source of new work.
:::
