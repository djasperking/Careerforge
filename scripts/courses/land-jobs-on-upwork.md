## 🧠 What clients really want

### The client's real question

A client reading proposals is asking: **"Can this person solve my problem, without making my life harder?"**

They look for four things:

1. **Relevance:** have you done something like this?
2. **Clarity:** do you understand what I need?
3. **Reliability:** will you communicate and deliver on time?
4. **Fit:** are you in my budget and tone?

> 📌 **Remember:** clients hire people who make them feel **safe and understood**, not the cheapest person.

::: check What do clients most want to feel?
+ That the freelancer understands the job and is reliable
- That the freelancer is the cheapest
- That the freelancer is very busy
= Trust beats price for most good clients.
:::

### What they see first

In the list, a client sees your **name and title**, the **first two lines** of your proposal, your **rate or bid**, and some profile signals. Many decisions are made from just that.

### Bad clients exist too

Some clients are unclear, unreasonable or unsafe. Your goal is not "any job", but **good jobs with good clients**.

::: check What part of your proposal is seen first in a list?
+ The first two lines
- The last paragraph
- Your whole portfolio
= Make them count.
:::

## 🔎 The job-fit test

Before you spend Connects, run the **job-fit test**. Apply only if you can answer **yes** to most:

1. **Can I really do this?** (skills and tools)
2. **Can I show something similar?** (a sample or a result)
3. **Is the scope clear?** (deliverables, deadline, budget)
4. **Is the budget fair for the work?**
5. **Does the client look real?** (verified payment, history, reviews)
6. **Did I read the whole post?** (including screening questions)

> 📌 **Remember:** a proposal to a job you can win is worth ten to jobs you cannot.

::: check Which job fits you best?
+ A job in your niche where you have a similar sample and the scope is clear
- A job that pays well but needs skills you do not have
- A vague job with no budget
= Apply where you can really deliver.
:::

### Pick your battles

- **Narrow competition:** newer posts, specific requirements, a niche you know.
- **Heavy competition:** very easy jobs with huge numbers of proposals.
- **Look for clients who invite:** an **invitation** to apply is a strong signal, and you still need to reply well.

### Red flags (skip)

- Asks for **free work** or a long unpaid "test".
- Wants to **move off Upwork** or pay outside the platform.
- **Unverified payment**, no history, and a rushed tone.
- Sends **links or files** you must open to see the brief.
- Wants **personal documents** or passwords.

::: check Which is a reason to skip a job?
+ The client asks you to chat and be paid outside Upwork
- The scope is clear
- The client has good reviews
= It breaks the rules and removes protection.
:::

## 🔌 Use Connects like a budget

**Connects** are what you spend to apply (check the **current cost per proposal** in the job post and Upwork's Help Centre).

### Plan, do not spray

Decide each week:

1. **How many Connects can I spend?**
2. **How many proposals is that?**
3. **Which jobs deserve them?**

> **Example:** if you have enough Connects for 10 proposals this week, aim for **10 well-matched, well-written ones**, not 10 rushed ones.

> 📌 **Remember:** 10 great proposals beat 40 weak ones, and save you money.

::: check Which approach to Connects is better?
+ Spend them on jobs you can win with strong proposals
- Spend them on every job quickly
- Never apply
= Treat Connects as a budget.
:::

### Track your numbers

Keep a simple sheet:

| Date | Job | Connects used | Reply? | Interview? | Hired? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Product descriptions | 8 | Yes | Yes | No |
| Tue | Social posts | 6 | No | No | No |

After 20 proposals, look: **reply rate, interview rate, hire rate**. Fix the weakest part.

### Do not buy Connects blindly

Only buy more if your proposals are getting **replies**, because that means the approach works. If you get **no replies at all**, fix the **profile and proposals** first.

::: check If you get no replies after 20 proposals, what should you do first?
+ Improve your profile and proposals
- Buy many more Connects
- Send the same message again
= Fix the cause before spending more.
:::

## ✍️ Write proposals that stand out

### The first two lines decide everything

Compare these openings:

- **Weak:** *"Hello, I am a hard-working freelancer with 3 years of experience and I am the best choice for your project."*
- **Strong:** *"You need 8 product descriptions that make your new skincare range sound as good as it feels. I have written for two beauty shops and can send samples."*

> 📌 **Remember:** open with the **client's problem** and a **specific proof**.

::: check Which opening is stronger?
- "I am the best freelancer for you."
+ "You need 8 skincare descriptions that sell. I wrote similar ones for two beauty shops."
- "Please hire me."
= It names the need and gives proof.
:::

### The 5-part proposal (around 120 to 170 words)

1. **Hook:** their need in your words.
2. **Plan:** 2 to 3 steps on how you will do it.
3. **Proof:** one relevant sample or result with a link.
4. **Question:** a smart question about the job.
5. **Next step:** make it easy to reply.

> **Example:**
> You need 8 product descriptions that make your skincare range sound as good as it feels. I would start by reading your brand notes, then draft all 8 in a warm, clear tone, and revise once. Here is a similar sample: [link]. Quick question: should I include ingredients in each description? If helpful, I can send two drafts first so you can see the style.

### Answer the screening questions well

Answer **every question** with real details. Short, specific answers beat long, vague ones.

### Edit before you send

- Read it **aloud**. Cut anything generic.
- Check **spelling and the client's name**.
- Make sure it **answers what they asked for**, not what you want to say.

::: check What should a smart question in a proposal do?
+ Show you thought about the job
- Ask for off-platform payment
- Ask them to reply immediately
= Good questions show expertise.
:::

## 🎤 Client messages and interviews

When a client replies, you are **close**. Do not lose it now.

### Reply fast and clearly

- Reply **within a few hours** if you can.
- Thank them, answer their questions **directly**, and ask **1 to 3** good questions.
- Keep messages **short, polite and professional**.

> 📌 **Remember:** speed and clarity show reliability, which is what clients are buying.

::: check Why reply quickly to a client message?
+ It shows you are reliable and keeps their interest
- It does not matter
- Speed is only for hourly jobs
= Clients often message several people.
:::

### Questions that win

- **"What does success look like for this project?"**
- **"Who is the audience?"**
- **"Do you have examples you like or a brand guide?"**
- **"What is your deadline and budget range?"**

### Handling "Why should I pick you?"

Answer with **relevance, process and reliability**:

> *"I have written product descriptions for two beauty shops, and I follow a simple process: brief, draft, one revision, then final files. I reply within a few hours, and can send two sample paragraphs first so you can check the tone."*

### Handling hard questions

- **"Can you lower the price?"** See the next lesson.
- **"Can you do a free sample?"** Offer a **small paid trial** or a **portfolio sample** instead of a custom free job.
- **"Can we talk on WhatsApp?"** Politely stay on the platform for protection.

::: check A client asks for a big free test project. What do you offer?
+ A portfolio sample, or a small paid trial
- A full unpaid project
- Nothing
= Protect your time.
:::

## 💰 Price and negotiate

### Price with a method

1. **Estimate hours** honestly, then add a **buffer**.
2. Decide the **hourly value** you need.
3. **Account for Upwork's current fee** and any withdrawal costs.
4. **Compare** with similar jobs, as a guide and not a copy.

> **Example:** 8 descriptions, about 45 minutes each, plus 2 hours revision and communication = about 8 hours. At 3,000 naira an hour, that is 24,000 naira before fees.

> 📌 **Remember:** the cheapest bid often attracts the worst clients. Price for the work **and the revisions**.

::: check 6 hours of work at 2,500 an hour is how much?
- 8,500
+ 15,000
- 6,000
= 6 x 2,500 = 15,000.
:::

### Beginner pricing strategy

- Your **first few jobs** can use a **slightly lower, fair** starter price, to earn reviews. Do not go so low that you cannot do a good job.
- After a few good reviews, **raise your rates** step by step.

### Negotiating politely

- **Lower the price** by lowering the **scope**: "For that budget I can write 6 descriptions instead of 8."
- Trade for something: **a longer deadline, fewer revisions, or a bigger order.**
- Never accept a **price that makes the job unfair to you**.
- Put the **final scope and price in writing** inside Upwork.

::: check How can you respond when the budget is lower than your price?
+ Offer a smaller scope for that budget
- Do all the work for less
- Argue
= Scope is a fair negotiating tool.
:::

## 🏁 Land and deliver your first jobs

### Your first-job plan

1. **Pick small, clear jobs** you can deliver excellently.
2. **Be fast and clear** in messages.
3. **Deliver a little early** when possible.
4. **Proofread** and double-check the brief.
5. **Ask politely for feedback** after the client is happy.

> 📌 **Remember:** early reviews open doors. A small job done really well is more valuable than a big job done badly.

::: check What is a smart way to start on Upwork?
+ Take small, clear jobs and deliver excellently
- Take huge jobs you cannot finish
- Ignore the brief
= Reputation starts with reliability.
:::

### During the job

- **Start with an update** within a day.
- **Share progress** (a draft or first section).
- **Confirm scope changes in writing**, and price extras fairly.
- Use **milestones** and **funded work** only.
- If something goes wrong, **tell the client early** and offer a fix.

### After the job

- **Deliver all files** clearly named.
- **Thank the client**, and ask for honest feedback.
- **Leave fair feedback** for the client.
- **Add the result** to your portfolio (with permission).

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** never ask for fake reviews, trade reviews, or pressure a client. It can harm your account.

::: check What is true about asking for reviews?
+ Ask politely for honest feedback after good work
- Pay for fake reviews
- Swap reviews to boost scores
= Honest reviews build real reputation.
:::

## 🔁 Turn clients into repeat business

**Repeat clients** are the easiest income to earn, because the trust is already there.

### Make repeat work likely

1. **Deliver reliably** and communicate early.
2. **Document what you learned** about their brand and preferences.
3. **Suggest the next step** ("Shall I plan next month's posts too?").
4. **Stay in touch** politely.
5. **Offer a simple package** for ongoing work (for example "10 posts a month").

> 📌 **Remember:** one happy client who returns is worth many new proposals.

::: check Why aim for repeat clients?
+ Trust is already built, so winning work is easier
- They always pay more
- It means you can stop learning
= Repeat work saves time and Connects.
:::

### Protect the relationship

- **Be honest** about delays, and **fix mistakes** fast.
- **Do not take clients off the platform.** Keep contracts inside Upwork.
- **Say no politely** to work outside your skills.

### Build your reputation

Keep your **profile current**, add **new samples**, and update your **title and overview** as you learn what clients respond to.

::: check How do you keep a client relationship healthy?
+ Be honest, fix mistakes fast and keep work on the platform
- Hide problems
- Move to private deals
= Trust and rules protect you.
:::

=== TEXT: 📈 Learn from what does not work | 480
## 📈 Learn from what does not work

Every freelancer loses proposals. The difference is that winners **learn from the losses**.

### Diagnose the problem

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No replies | Weak first lines, wrong jobs, weak profile | Rewrite openings, target better jobs, improve profile |
| Replies but no interviews | Price or fit unclear | Clarify fit and offer, check price |
| Interviews but no hires | Weak answers, trust gap, price | Practise answers, add proof, adjust scope |
| Hired but poor reviews | Unclear scope or weak delivery | Confirm scope, improve process |

> 📌 **Remember:** fix **one thing at a time** and track the result.

::: check You get replies but no interviews. What should you check first?
+ Your fit, offer clarity and price
- Whether to quit
- Whether to buy a new account
= Look at the stage where people drop off.
:::

### Keep learning

- Study **successful proposals** (yours and, from public examples, others'). Never copy.
- Learn **one new skill** or tool that clients in your niche want.
- Check **Upwork's updates** and guidelines regularly.
- Take care of yourself: freelancing is a **marathon**.

### If you are stuck

After **30 well-matched proposals** without success, **review your profile, samples and niche** with a friend or mentor, adjust, and try again. Persistence with improvement beats giving up or repeating the same approach.

::: check What should you do after many unsuccessful, well-matched proposals?
+ Review your profile, samples and niche, then adjust
- Send the same proposal again
- Create a second account
= Change something, and keep improving.
:::
