## 📐 Structure and layout

### The sections, in order

1. **Header:** name, phone, professional email, city, LinkedIn link.
2. **Summary:** 2 to 3 lines about who you are and what you offer.
3. **Experience:** jobs, internships, volunteering, projects (newest first).
4. **Education and training.**
5. **Skills:** the tools and abilities that match the job.

> 📌 **Remember:** put the most relevant, most recent information at the top.

::: check Which comes first in your experience section?
- Your oldest job
+ Your newest or most relevant role
- Your hobbies
= Reverse-chronological order is the standard.
:::

### Layout rules

- **One page** if you have under 10 years of experience.
- A clear font (Calibri, Arial) at size 10 to 12.
- Plenty of white space and consistent headings.
- Save and send as a **PDF** named like `Ada-Obi-CV.pdf`, unless the employer asks for another format.
- Use a **professional email** like `ada.obi@gmail.com`, not `sweetgirl99@...`.

::: check Which file format should you usually send?
+ PDF
- A photo of the page
- A screenshot
= PDFs keep your layout and are easy to read.
:::

### No experience? Use what you have

Include **projects**, **volunteering**, **courses and certificates** (like your Career Forge ones), leadership roles and **skills you have proven**.

> 💡 **Tip:** a student with two good projects can beat a person with a vague job list.

::: check What can you include if you have no job experience?
- Nothing
+ Projects, volunteering, courses and proven skills
- Made-up jobs
= Real evidence of skill counts, and never invent experience.
:::

## ✂️ Drill 1: what belongs on a CV

A CV is **short**, so every line must earn its place. Include proof of your skills. Leave off anything that does not help the employer decide.

### The drill

::: drill Include or leave off
labels: Include | Leave off
rule: Include what proves your skills and lets the employer contact you. Leave off personal details that are not needed, filler lines and anything unrelated to the job.
pass: 80
- A professional email address like ada.obi@gmail.com. => Include | Employers need to contact you, and it looks professional.
- Your age and religion. => Leave off | These are not needed to judge your skills.
- A relevant volunteer project with results. => Include | It is proof of skill.
- Your marital status. => Leave off | It is not relevant to the job.
- Your phone number and city. => Include | They let employers reach you.
- The line "References available on request". => Leave off | It wastes space, since employers will ask.
- Relevant certificates and courses, such as a Career Forge certificate. => Include | They show real, recent learning.
- A photo, unless the employer asks for one. => Leave off | In many countries it is not expected and can cause bias.
- A list of unrelated hobbies like movies and music. => Leave off | It does not help for this role.
- A link to your portfolio or LinkedIn profile. => Include | It lets them see more proof.
:::

> 📌 **Remember:** if a line does not help you get the interview, cut it.

## 💪 Drill 2: bullet points that show results

Weak CVs list **duties**. Strong CVs show **results**.

### The formula

**Action verb + what you did + result (with a number if possible).**

- **Weak:** "Responsible for social media."
- **Strong:** "Grew Instagram followers from 400 to 2,100 in 4 months by posting 3 times a week."

> 📌 **Remember:** start with a strong action verb, and show the result.

::: check Which bullet is strongest?
- Responsible for sales
+ Increased weekly sales by 25% by introducing a loyalty card
- Did sales stuff
= Numbers and results prove your impact.
:::

### Action verbs to use

*Led, built, organised, reduced, increased, trained, designed, saved, launched, improved, solved.*

### No numbers? Be specific

Honest estimates are fine: "Handled about 30 customer enquiries a day", "Trained 5 new staff". Never invent numbers you cannot defend in an interview.

### The drill

::: drill Strong or weak bullet
labels: Strong | Weak
rule: A strong bullet starts with an action verb, says what you did, and shows a result or a concrete detail. A weak bullet only lists a duty, or is vague.
pass: 80
- Responsible for social media. => Weak | It lists a duty, with no result.
- Grew Instagram followers from 400 to 2,100 in 4 months by posting 3 times a week. => Strong | Action, method and a measurable result.
- Helped customers. => Weak | Vague, with no detail.
- Resolved 25+ customer enquiries daily by phone and WhatsApp, keeping replies under 10 minutes. => Strong | Specific numbers and a result.
- Worked in a team. => Weak | It says nothing about what you did.
- Cut stock-count errors by 30% by introducing a weekly checklist. => Strong | Shows impact and method.
- Duties included cleaning and organising. => Weak | A list of duties.
- Trained 5 new staff in the till system in one week. => Strong | Concrete and measurable.
- Handled many tasks. => Weak | Too vague.
- Raised 150,000 naira for a school project by organising a charity sale. => Strong | Action and a clear result.
:::

### Practice: rewrite this

*"Helped customers."*

Better: *"Resolved 25+ customer enquiries daily by phone and WhatsApp, keeping replies under 10 minutes."*

::: check If you cannot find a number, what should you ask yourself?
+ How many, how often, how fast, or how much did it save?
- Whether to give up
- Whether to invent one
= Honest estimates and details still make bullets stronger.
:::

## 📝 Drill 3: summary and skills

### A short summary

Two or three lines at the top: **who you are**, **what you do well**, and **what you want**.

> *Detail-focused customer support graduate with 1 year of volunteering experience. Fast, polite and reliable in writing and on calls. Looking for a remote support role.*

> 📌 **Remember:** a good summary names your strengths and your target role, with proof where possible.

### The drill

::: drill Better summary
labels: Option A | Option B
rule: Choose the summary that is specific, names a strength with proof, and names the role wanted. Avoid empty words like hardworking, passionate and go-getter.
pass: 75
- A: Hardworking person looking for a good opportunity to grow. B: Detail-focused customer support graduate with one year of volunteering experience, fast and polite in writing and on calls, seeking a remote support role. => Option B | It is specific and names the role.
- A: I am a very passionate go-getter and team player who thinks outside the box. B: Bookkeeping assistant with 2 years of experience reconciling accounts and producing weekly reports with under 1% errors. => Option B | It gives proof instead of buzzwords.
- A: Marketing graduate who grew a school club's Instagram from 300 to 3,000 followers, seeking a social media role. B: Looking for any job. => Option A | It shows a result and a target.
- A: Reliable, honest and willing to learn. B: Junior web developer who built three responsive websites with HTML, CSS and JavaScript, seeking a front-end internship. => Option B | It shows skills and a clear goal.
:::

### Skills that match the job

List **hard skills** (Excel, Canva, customer support tools, HTML) and a few **soft skills** that you prove through examples. Copy the **exact words** from the job post when they are true for you.

### What not to include

- Skills you cannot demonstrate.
- Spelling or grammar mistakes. Proofread twice and ask a friend.

::: check Which should you leave off a CV?
+ A skill you cannot demonstrate
- A tool you have used well
- A result you achieved
= Claim only what you can prove.
:::

## 🎯 Drill 4: tailoring and screening software

Many companies use an **Applicant Tracking System (ATS)** that scans CVs for keywords before a person sees them.

### How to tailor

1. Read the **job description** closely.
2. Highlight the **skills and phrases** it repeats.
3. Reorder your CV so the **most relevant** items come first.
4. Use the **same words** where they honestly apply.

> 📌 **Remember:** a CV tailored to the job beats a generic CV every time.

### The drill

::: drill ATS-friendly or risky
labels: ATS-friendly | Risky
rule: ATS-friendly means simple, standard and readable by software. Risky means layouts or tricks that software or people cannot read, or that are dishonest.
pass: 75
- Simple headings: Experience, Education, Skills. => ATS-friendly | Software recognises them.
- Text boxes and tables for the whole layout. => Risky | Software may skip or jumble them.
- A single-column layout in a standard font. => ATS-friendly | Easy to read.
- A photo and icons in the header. => Risky | They can confuse screening.
- Creative headings like My Journey and My Superpowers. => Risky | Software looks for standard headings.
- Keywords from the job post, used honestly. => ATS-friendly | This matches the job.
- Saving as PDF or Word, as the employer asks. => ATS-friendly | Follow the instructions.
- Skills hidden in tiny white text. => Risky | Dishonest, and often detected.
:::

### Be honest

Never claim a skill you do not have. You will be asked about it in the interview, and employers check.

::: check Why tailor your CV to each job?
+ It matches the words and needs of that employer
- It makes the CV longer
- It is only for managers
= Targeted CVs pass screening more often.
:::

## 💼 Drill 5: a LinkedIn profile that gets found

Recruiters search LinkedIn every day. A complete profile helps them find **you**.

### The parts that matter most

1. **Photo:** clear, friendly, plain background, face filling about 60% of the frame.
2. **Headline:** not just "Student". Say what you do.
3. **About:** 3 to 5 short lines on who you are, what you do well and what you want.
4. **Experience:** the same strong bullets as your CV.
5. **Skills:** 10 to 20 relevant ones.
6. **Featured:** link your best project or your **Career Forge certificate**.

> 📌 **Remember:** your headline and photo are the first things people see. Make them clear.

### The drill

::: drill Better headline
labels: Option A | Option B
rule: Choose the headline that says what you do and includes the keywords recruiters search for. Vague headlines are weak.
pass: 75
- A: Student. B: Customer support graduate | Chat and email | Excel | Remote work seeker. => Option B | It names the role and keywords.
- A: Hardworking person. B: Social media assistant | Content planning | Canva | Small business growth. => Option B | It says what you do.
- A: Looking for opportunities. B: Virtual assistant for coaches | Inbox, calendar and admin. => Option B | It is specific and searchable.
- A: Junior web developer | HTML, CSS, JavaScript | Building responsive sites. B: Developer. => Option A | It is specific and has keywords.
:::

### Grow your network

- Connect with **classmates, colleagues and people in your field**, with a short note.
- **Follow** companies you like.
- Comment helpfully on posts. Visibility builds trust.

### Be safe

Never share your password, and do not trust recruiters who ask for money.

::: check Which is a good way to grow your network?

## ✉️ Drill 6: the application note

### Keep it short

An application note is **3 short paragraphs**:

1. **Why this job:** name the role and the company.
2. **Why you:** two proof points that match the job.
3. **Next step:** thank them and say you are available.

> **Example:**
> Hello Mr Bello, I am applying for the Customer Support role at BrightPay. In my last role I answered 30+ chats a day and kept response time under 10 minutes. I am calm with upset customers and keep accurate records. I would love to talk. Thank you, Ada

### The drill

::: drill Better opening
labels: Option A | Option B
rule: Choose the opening that names the role, gives a specific proof and sounds professional. Avoid generic lines and begging.
pass: 75
- A: To whom it may concern, I am writing to apply for any job. B: Hello Ms Bello, I am applying for the Customer Support role at BrightPay. In my last role I answered 30+ chats a day. => Option B | It names the role and gives proof.
- A: I am the best candidate and you will not regret hiring me. B: I noticed you need someone calm with upset customers. At Bright Mart I resolved complaints daily and kept replies quick and polite. => Option B | It links your proof to their need.
- A: Dear Hiring Manager, I am applying for the Data Entry Clerk role. I type at 55 words per minute with 98% accuracy and have used Excel for two years. B: Please consider me. => Option A | It is specific and relevant.
- A: I need money urgently, please give me the job. B: I am applying for the Sales Assistant role. I have two years of retail experience and consistently met weekly sales targets. => Option B | It focuses on value to the employer.
:::

### Applying well

- Apply on the **company's own website** or official page.
- Follow **instructions exactly** (file name, subject line, questions).
- **Proofread** and check the contact name.
- Keep a **list** of every application: date, role, company, status.
- **Follow up** politely after about a week.

::: check What should you do right after you apply?
- Nothing ever
+ Record it and follow up politely after about a week
- Send the CV to 100 more people
= A tracker and a polite follow-up show you are serious.
:::

## 🎤 Drill 7: interviews and the STAR method

### Before the interview

1. **Research** the company: what they do, who they serve.
2. Re-read the **job description** and your CV.
3. Prepare **three stories** that show your skills.
4. Test your **device, internet and video link** early. Dress neatly.

### Common questions

- **"Tell me about yourself."** Give 3 sentences: who you are, what you do well, what you want. Keep it to the job.
- **"Why do you want this job?"** Link their needs to your strengths.
- **"What is a weakness?"** Name a real, small one and say how you are improving it.
- **"Tell me about a time you solved a problem."** Use **STAR**.

### The STAR method

- **S**ituation: the context.
- **T**ask: what needed doing.
- **A**ction: what **you** did.
- **R**esult: what happened.

> 📌 **Remember:** STAR keeps stories clear: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Focus on **your** actions.

### The drill

::: drill Which part of STAR?
labels: Situation | Task | Action | Result
rule: Situation is the context. Task is what you had to do. Action is what you did. Result is what happened because of it.
pass: 75
- Our shop was losing customers because replies on WhatsApp took a day. => Situation | It sets the scene.
- I was asked to bring reply times down within a month. => Task | It is the goal you were given.
- I created reply templates and checked messages every two hours. => Action | It is what you did.
- Average reply time dropped from 24 hours to 2 hours and complaints fell by half. => Result | It is the outcome.
- I was responsible for the club's events budget during a busy term. => Task | Your responsibility.
- I compared three suppliers and negotiated a 15% discount. => Action | What you did.
- The club saved 40,000 naira and we hosted two extra events. => Result | The outcome.
- A key volunteer left a week before our biggest event. => Situation | The background.
:::

### Ask questions

Prepare **two questions** for them: "What does success look like in the first three months?" It shows interest.

::: check Why prepare questions for the interviewer?
+ It shows genuine interest and helps you decide
- It is rude
- It is required by law
= Good questions leave a strong impression.
:::

## 🔒 Drill 8: job-hunt scams and your tracker

### Scam signs

- They ask you to **pay** a fee, deposit or "training cost".
- They promise **guaranteed income**.
- They contact you first on **WhatsApp or Telegram** and want to leave the platform.
- They send a **cheque** and ask you to send some back.
- They ask for your **bank PIN, password or OTP**.
- The email comes from a **free address** that does not match the company.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** real employers never ask you to pay to be hired.

### The drill

::: drill Scam check
labels: Looks real | Scam | Check first
rule: Scam: asks for money, PINs or passwords, or promises something too good to be true. Looks real: no payment, an official channel and realistic claims. Check first: could be real, but you must verify it.
pass: 75
- Your CV looks great! Pay 5,000 naira for our priority placement. => Scam | Real employers do not charge you.
- We would like to invite you for an interview on our official video platform. No payment is needed. => Check first | Verify the company and the link.
- Send your bank PIN to verify your identity for payroll. => Scam | No one legitimate needs your PIN.
- Apply through the careers page on the company's official website. => Looks real | It uses an official channel. Still check the web address.
- You are hired! Buy a laptop from our supplier and we will refund you. => Scam | A classic fake-employer scam.
- A recruiter on LinkedIn with a complete profile messages you about a role. => Check first | Check their profile and the company's website.
:::

### Your job tracker

| Date | Company | Role | Where applied | Status | Follow up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 Oct | BrightPay | Support agent | Company site | Applied | 12 Oct |
| 6 Oct | ShopNest | Data entry | Job board | Interview 14 Oct | 15 Oct |

> 📌 **Remember:** a tracker keeps you organised and stops you missing follow-ups.

::: check Why keep a job tracker?
+ It keeps applications and follow-ups organised
- It guarantees interviews
- It is required by employers
= Organisation raises your chances.
:::
