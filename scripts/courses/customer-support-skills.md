## ⭐ What great support looks like

### The five qualities

1. **Empathy:** show you understand how the customer feels.
2. **Clarity:** simple words, short sentences, no jargon.
3. **Speed:** answer quickly, and set honest expectations.
4. **Ownership:** take responsibility until it is solved.
5. **Accuracy:** give the right information, and say "let me check" when you are unsure.

> 📌 **Remember:** customers forgive problems, but they do not forgive being ignored.

::: check What do customers remember most?
+ Whether they felt heard and helped
- Your typing speed
- The font in your email
= Feeling cared for matters most.
:::

### Phrases that help

- "I understand how frustrating that is."
- "Thank you for your patience. I am looking into it now."
- "I am sorry for the trouble. Let me fix this."
- "Let me check that and come back to you within 10 minutes."

### Phrases that hurt

- "That is not my job."
- "It is your fault."
- "Calm down."
- "There is nothing I can do."

Instead say: **"Here is what I can do..."**

::: check What is better than "There is nothing I can do"?
- "Not my problem"
+ "Here is what I can do..."
- Nothing
= Always offer a next step.
:::

## 🏢 A day in a support job

### The usual rhythm

1. **Check the queue:** new tickets, chats and calls.
2. **Handle urgent items first.**
3. **Reply, solve or escalate** each ticket.
4. **Update notes and statuses.**

6. **Review your quality feedback.**

### Where support happens

- **Email and tickets:** structured, with time to think.
- **Live chat and WhatsApp:** fast and conversational.
- **Phone:** calm voice and good listening.
- **Social media:** public, so tone matters even more.

### Skills employers look for

- Clear **writing**
- **Calm** under pressure
- **Learning** new tools quickly
- **Reliability** (showing up, meeting targets)
- **Honesty** about what you do not know

> 📌 **Remember:** a support agent is the **voice of the company**. Every reply is read by a real person who is deciding how they feel about the brand.

## ✍️ Drill 1: choose the better reply

Most support is written, so your writing is your product.

### The reply formula

1. **Greet** using the name.
2. **Acknowledge** the problem (empathy).
3. **Solve** with clear steps, or say what happens next and when.
4. **Close** with a polite offer to help more.

> **Example:**
> Hello Ada, I am sorry your order has not arrived. I checked and it is with our rider and will reach you by 5pm today. I will message you when it is delivered. Is there anything else I can help with? Kind regards, Tunde

> 📌 **Remember:** greet, acknowledge, solve, close.

::: check What comes right after the greeting in a good reply?
- Your sign-off
+ Acknowledging the customer's problem
- A long apology essay
= Show you understood before you solve.
:::

### Writing rules

- **Short sentences**, simple words.
- **Numbered steps** for instructions.
- **Specific times** ("by 5pm today") instead of "soon".
- **Positive wording:** "I can help with that" not "We cannot do that".
- **Proofread** before sending.

### The drill

::: drill Pick the better reply
labels: Reply A | Reply B
rule: Choose the reply that is polite, specific, takes ownership and gives the customer a next step.
pass: 70
- Customer: My order is late. A: Wait. B: I am sorry about the delay, Ada. I checked and it arrives today by 5pm, and I will message you when it is delivered. => Reply B | It apologises, gives a specific time and promises an update.
- Customer: The app crashed. A: That is not our fault, update your phone. B: Sorry for the trouble. Could you tell me your phone model and app version so I can fix this? => Reply B | It takes ownership and asks for what is needed.
- Customer: How do I reset my password? A: Tap Forgot password on the login screen, enter your email and follow the link we send. B: Read the help page. => Reply A | It gives clear steps.
- Customer: Can I get a refund? A: We cannot do anything. B: I can help with that. Please send your order number so I can check if it qualifies and tell you the steps. => Reply B | It offers help and a next step.
- Customer: Thanks for your help! A: Anytime! Message us again if you need anything. B: ok => Reply A | It is warm and invites them back.
- Customer: I was charged twice! A: Please send your PIN so I can check. B: I am sorry. I will check this with our payments team. Please share the order number and the time of the charges. Never send us your PIN. => Reply B | It never asks for a PIN, and gives a clear next step.
:::

> 📌 **Remember:** a good reply is polite, specific and gives a next step. Never ask for a PIN or password.

## 🤝 Drill 2: empathy and tone

### Empathy is not agreeing with everything

Empathy means showing you **understand the feeling**. You can do this **without admitting fault** or promising what you cannot deliver.

- "I can see why that is frustrating."
- "I understand this is urgent for you."
- "Thank you for telling us. I will look into it now."

### Tone checklist

1. Use the customer's **name**.
2. Use **warm, plain language**.
3. Avoid **blame** ("you should have...").
4. Avoid **jargon** and **robot phrases**.
5. Keep **capital letters and exclamation marks** under control.

### The drill

::: drill Good or poor response
labels: Good response | Poor response
rule: A good response shows empathy, avoids blame and offers a next step. A poor response dismisses, blames or leaves the customer stuck.
pass: 75
- I understand how frustrating that is. Let me fix it. => Good response | It shows empathy and offers action.
- Calm down. => Poor response | It dismisses the customer's feeling.
- That is not my job. => Poor response | It leaves the customer stuck.
- Thank you for your patience. I am looking into it now. => Good response | Polite, and promises action.
- It is your fault, you did not read the terms. => Poor response | It blames the customer.
- I am sorry for the trouble. Here is what I can do: refund the extra charge today. => Good response | It apologises and offers a clear solution.
- There is nothing I can do. => Poor response | It offers no alternative.
- Let me check that for you and come back within 10 minutes. => Good response | It sets a clear, honest expectation.
:::

### Rewrite practice

*"No refunds. Read the policy."* could become: *"I am sorry this did not work out. Our policy allows refunds within 7 days. Your order is 10 days old, so I cannot refund it, but I can offer you a 10% discount code or an exchange. Which would you like?"*

::: check What makes the rewrite better?
+ It explains, apologises and offers alternatives
- It is longer
- It breaks the rules
= It stays honest and still helps.
:::

## 🧘 Handling upset customers

Anger is usually about the **problem**, not about you.

### The calm method: LAST

- **L**isten fully (or read the whole message) without interrupting.
- **A**pologise for the experience.
- **S**olve the problem or give a clear plan.
- **T**hank them for their patience.

> 📌 **Remember:** listen, apologise, solve, thank, in that order.

::: check What does the A in LAST stand for?
- Argue
+ Apologise
- Avoid
= A sincere apology lowers tension.
:::

### Tips

- **Do not take it personally.**
- **Never argue**, even if the customer is wrong.
- Use their **name**, and slow, calm wording.
- **Repeat their issue back** to show you understood.
- If you cannot fix it, **escalate**, and tell the customer what happens next.

### Worked example

*Customer: "This is useless! My payment failed twice and I am still charged!"*

**Weak reply:** "That is not our fault. Try again."

**Strong reply:** "I am really sorry, Mr Eze. Being charged without getting your order is frustrating. I have flagged this with our payments team. I will confirm the result by email within 24 hours, and a refund will be made if the charge is confirmed."

::: check What is the best first move with an angry customer?
+ Listen and apologise for the experience
- Tell them to calm down
- Close the ticket
= Empathy comes before solutions.
:::

### Threats and abuse

If a customer threatens or abuses you, stay **calm and professional**, warn **once** that you will end the chat if it continues, and **follow your team's policy**. Your safety and wellbeing matter.

::: check What should you do if a customer becomes abusive?
+ Stay calm, warn once, and follow the policy
- Insult them back
- Ignore the policy
= Professionalism protects you and the company.
:::

## ☎️ Chat, email and phone

### Email

- Clear **subject line**, full greeting, structured steps, polite close.
- Reply within the promised time, and set expectations if it takes longer.

### Live chat and WhatsApp

- **Reply fast** and keep messages short.
- **One message per idea**, not one giant block.
- Say when you are **checking something**: "One moment while I look this up."
- Use quick-reply templates, but **personalise** them.

### Phone

- **Smile** (it changes your voice), speak slowly and clearly.
- Take **notes** while the customer speaks.
- **Repeat key details back** (names, numbers, order IDs).
- Never leave long silences without saying why.

> 📌 **Remember:** match the channel: short and quick on chat, structured on email, calm and clear on the phone.

::: check Which is right for live chat?
+ Short messages, fast replies, personalised
- One huge paragraph
- No reply for hours
= Chat is fast and conversational.
:::

### Privacy and safety

- **Verify** who you are talking to before sharing account information.
- **Never** ask for full card numbers, PINs or passwords.
- Do not share one customer's data with another.

::: check What should you never ask a customer for?
- Their order number
+ Their PIN or password
- Their name
= Support should never need PINs or passwords.
:::

## 🏷️ Drill 3: priority and routing

A queue is never empty. **Triage** means deciding **what to do first**, and **who should handle it**.

### Priority levels (an example)

- **Urgent:** money lost, a security risk, or the customer cannot use the service at all.
- **High:** a paid feature is broken, or an order is badly late.
- **Normal:** standard questions and small issues.
- **Low:** feedback and suggestions with no problem.

> 📌 **Remember:** handle **urgent** items first, even if they arrived last.

- A request for a dark mode

## 🧩 Drill 4: answer, ask or escalate

### The three moves

- **Answer now:** you know the answer from the knowledge base or experience.
- **Ask for more information:** you cannot tell what is wrong yet.
- **Escalate:** it needs another team, involves lost money or security, or you do not have permission.

> 📌 **Remember:** never guess. Asking or escalating is better than a wrong answer.

::: check The customer says only "It does not work." What is the best move?
+ Ask for more information
- Guess the cause
- Close the ticket
= You need details to help.
:::

### A good question

- **Specific:** "What message do you see when you press Pay?"
- **One or two at a time**, not ten.
- **Polite:** "To help me find the problem, could you share..."

### A good escalation note

Include: **who** the customer is, **what happened**, **what you tried**, **what you need**. A good handover prevents the customer from repeating themselves.

### The drill

::: drill What do you do?
labels: Answer now | Ask for more info | Escalate
rule: Answer now if the knowledge base answers it. Ask for more info if you cannot tell what is wrong. Escalate if it needs another team, involves lost money or a security risk, or you lack permission.

- Where is the Settings menu? => Answer now | A simple, known answer.
- It does not work. => Ask for more info | You cannot tell what is wrong.
- Someone used my account to place orders I did not make. => Escalate | A security risk that needs another team.
- How long does a refund take? => Answer now | A policy answer from the knowledge base.
- The app shows an error. => Ask for more info | You need the message and steps.
- I was charged but the order failed. => Escalate | Lost money that the payments team must check.

## 🧩 A step-by-step problem-solving method

### The five steps

1. **Understand:** read the whole message. Ask questions if it is unclear.
2. **Gather:** order number, account email, device, screenshots, what they tried.
3. **Diagnose:** find the likely cause using your knowledge base.
4. **Fix:** apply the solution, or escalate with full details.
5. **Confirm:** check it worked, and thank the customer.

> 📌 **Remember:** understand, gather, diagnose, fix, confirm. Never skip the last step.

::: check What is the last step of the method?
- Fix
+ Confirm it worked
- Close without checking
= Confirming avoids repeat tickets.
:::

### Example: "I cannot log in"

1. **Understand:** is it the password, the email or an error message?
2. **Gather:** account email, device, the exact message.
3. **Diagnose:** wrong password? Locked account? Outage?
4. **Fix:** guide a password reset, or escalate if the account is locked.
5. **Confirm:** "Are you able to log in now?"

### Close the loop

After a fix, **say what was wrong and what to do if it happens again**. It turns a problem into confidence.

::: check Why explain what went wrong after a fix?
+ It helps the customer avoid the problem next time
- It wastes time
- It is not allowed
= Good support teaches, not just fixes.
:::

## 🛠️ Tickets, macros and the knowledge base

### Ticketing tools

Support teams use tools like **Zendesk, Freshdesk, Help Scout** or even shared inboxes. They turn each message into a **ticket** with a status: *New, Open, Pending, Solved*.

- Always **update the status** and add **internal notes**.
- Use **tags** so the team can spot patterns (for example "payment issue").
- Set **priority** honestly.

> 📌 **Remember:** notes and tags help the next person (and you in a week) understand the case.

::: check Why add internal notes to a ticket?
+ So colleagues understand the history
- To hide mistakes
- To confuse customers
= Good notes make teamwork smooth.
:::

### Macros (saved replies)

A **macro** is a saved reply for common questions. They save time, but always **personalise**: use the name and the details.

> **A good macro:**
> Hello {name}, thank you for contacting us. I understand you were charged twice for order {order_number}. I am sorry about that. I have sent this to our payments team, and you will hear back within 24 hours. If you have a screenshot of the charges, please reply with it. Kind regards, {agent}

### The knowledge base

A **knowledge base** is a library of help articles. A good article has a **clear title**, **numbered steps**, **screenshots or examples**, and **what to do if it does not work**.

> **Example article: "How to reset your password"**
> 1. Tap **Forgot password** on the login screen.
> 2. Enter the email you used to register.
> 3. Open the email we send and tap the link.
> 4. Choose a new password with at least 8 characters.
> If you do not get the email within 10 minutes, check your spam folder or contact support.

::: check What makes a good help article?
+ A clear title, numbered steps and what to do if it fails
- Long paragraphs with no steps
- Only a title
= Customers want to solve it themselves quickly.
:::

### Improve the system

If many customers ask the same thing, tell your team. Fixing the cause or the article is better than answering 100 times.

::: check What should you do when many customers ask the same question?
- Ignore it
+ Tell the team so the cause or the help article can be fixed
- Argue with them
= Support finds patterns that help the whole business.
:::

## 📈 Quality and your performance

### Common measures

- **First response time:** how quickly you reply first.
- **Resolution time:** how long it takes to solve.
- **CSAT:** customer satisfaction score, from a short survey.
- **First contact resolution:** solved on the first reply.
- **Quality review score:** a manager checks tone, accuracy and process.

> 📌 **Remember:** speed matters, but a fast wrong answer is worse than a slightly slower right one.

::: check What does CSAT measure?
+ How satisfied customers are
- How fast you type
- How many tickets are open
= It is the customer's own rating.
:::

### A simple quality scorecard

Managers often score replies from 1 to 5 on:

| Area | What they look for |
|---|---|
| **Empathy** | Did you acknowledge the feeling? |
| **Accuracy** | Was the information right? |
| **Clarity** | Easy to read, with clear steps? |
| **Ownership** | Did you solve it or hand over properly? |
| **Tone and grammar** | Polite, correct, professional? |

### Improving

1. Read your **lowest-rated** conversations and learn from them.
2. Copy the habits of top performers.
3. Keep a **notes file** of tricky cases.
4. Ask for **feedback** and use it.

### Protect yourself

Take **breaks**, and do not take rude customers home with you. Ask for support after a hard conversation.

::: check What is a good way to improve?
- Never read feedback
+ Learn from low-rated conversations and top performers
- Work faster at any cost
= Reflect and practise.
:::

## 🧰 Support cheat sheet

### Reply formula

**Greet, acknowledge, solve, close.**

### Upset customer

**LAST:** Listen, Apologise, Solve, Thank.

**Understand, gather, diagnose, fix, confirm.**

### Triage
