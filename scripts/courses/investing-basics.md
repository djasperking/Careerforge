## ⚖️ Saving vs investing, risk vs return

### Saving and investing are different jobs

- **Saving:** keeps money **safe and available** (emergencies, near goals). It earns little, but you do not expect to lose it.
- **Investing:** aims for **growth over years**, and accepts the chance of loss or ups and downs.

> 📌 **Remember:** short-term goals (under about 3 years) belong in savings. Long-term goals can use investments.

::: check Where should money for an emergency next month be kept?
+ In savings you can reach quickly
- In a risky investment
- Lent to a friend
= Emergency money must be safe and available.
:::

### Risk and return go together

**Higher possible return = higher risk.** An offer that promises **high returns with no risk** is a red flag.

| Type | Typical risk | Typical return potential |
|---|---|---|
| Savings account | Very low | Low |
| Government treasury bills | Low | Low to moderate |
| Bonds and money market funds | Low to moderate | Moderate |
| Stocks (company shares) | High, with ups and downs | Higher over the long term |
| New or unregulated schemes | Very high, often fraud | "Promised" but unreliable |

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** the table shows general ideas, and actual rates and risks change. Always check current information.

::: check Which statement is true?
+ Higher possible returns usually come with higher risk
- High returns with zero risk are normal
- Risk disappears after a year
= Risk and return are linked.
:::

### Inflation: why not just keep cash?

**Inflation** means prices rise over time, so the same money buys less. If your savings grow slower than inflation, you lose **buying power**. That is one reason people invest for the long term.

::: check What does inflation do to cash kept for years?
+ Reduces what it can buy
- Increases what it can buy
- Nothing
= Prices rise, so each naira buys less.
:::

## 📈 How money grows: interest and compounding

### Simple interest

Interest paid only on the **original** amount.

> 100,000 at 10% a year simple interest earns **10,000** every year.

### Compound interest

Interest earned on the **original amount plus past interest**. It grows faster over time.

> 100,000 at 10% a year, compounded yearly:
> Year 1: 110,000, Year 2: 121,000, Year 3: 133,100.

::: check 100,000 at 10% compounded yearly is how much after 2 years?
- 120,000
+ 121,000
- 110,000
= Year 1: 110,000. Year 2: 110,000 x 1.10 = 121,000.
:::

> 📌 **Remember:** compounding rewards **time**. Starting early and staying invested matters more than starting big.

### The rule of 72 (quick estimate)

Divide **72 by the yearly return** to estimate how many years it takes to **double**.

- At 8% a year: 72 / 8 = about **9 years**
- At 12% a year: 72 / 12 = about **6 years**

::: check Using the rule of 72, about how long to double money at 6% a year?
- 6 years
+ 12 years
- 20 years
= 72 divided by 6 is 12.
:::

### Returns are not smooth

Real investments **go up and down**. A good long-term average can include bad years. Never assume a steady rate like in examples.

## 🏦 Savings, fixed deposits and treasury bills

These are **lower-risk, lower-return** options for money you will need within months to a few years.

### Savings accounts

Easy access, little growth. Good for emergencies and short goals. Check that your bank is **licensed** and your deposit is covered by the national **deposit insurance** scheme (the limits are set by the regulator, so check the current ones).

### Fixed deposits

You lock money for a **set period** at an agreed rate. Breaking the lock early may cost you interest.

### Treasury bills (T-bills)

Short-term loans to the government, sold at a **discount** and paid back at **face value**.

> **Example:** you pay 95,000 now for a bill that pays 100,000 in a year. The 5,000 difference is your return (about 5.3%).

> 📌 **Remember:** "safer" does not mean "risk-free". Check rates, fees, lock-in periods and whether the provider is **properly licensed**.

::: check In a fixed deposit, what is the main trade-off?
+ Your money is locked for a period, in return for an agreed rate
- You can withdraw anytime with no limits
- There is no agreement
= Access for return.
:::

::: check A treasury bill is sold for 95,000 and pays 100,000 at maturity. What is the return in naira?
- 95,000
+ 5,000
- 100,000
= 100,000 minus 95,000 is 5,000.
:::

## 🏛️ Bonds and mutual funds

### Bonds

A bond is a **loan** to a government or company. They pay **interest** (the coupon) and return your money at the end, if the borrower can pay. **Companies can fail to repay**, so company bonds carry more risk than government ones.

### Mutual funds and money market funds

A **fund** pools money from **many people**, and **professional managers** invest it in a mix of assets. You own units of the fund.

- **Money market fund:** invests in short-term, low-risk instruments.
- **Bond fund:** invests mostly in bonds.
- **Equity fund:** invests in company shares (higher risk).

> 📌 **Remember:** funds give you **instant diversification** and professional management, but have **fees**, and values can fall.

::: check What is a mutual fund?
+ A pool of many investors' money managed by professionals
- A savings account
- A loan from a friend
= Investors share the fund's results.
:::

### Always ask

1. **Is the fund manager registered** with the regulator (the SEC in Nigeria)?
2. **What are the fees?**
3. **What does it invest in?**
4. **What is the past performance?** (It does not guarantee the future.)

::: check Does past performance guarantee future results?
- Yes
+ No
- Only for funds
= It is information, not a promise.
:::

## 🏢 Stocks and real estate

### Stocks (shares)

Buying a share means owning a **small piece of a company**. You may earn from:

1. **Price growth:** the share price rises.
2. **Dividends:** the company shares profit.

Prices **rise and fall daily**. Over many years, stocks have often grown more than safer assets, but there is **no guarantee**, and you can lose money, especially over short periods.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** do not invest in a single company because of a rumour or hype. Learn about the business, or use a diversified fund.

::: check What is a share?
+ A small piece of ownership in a company
- A loan to a company
- A government bill
= You become a part-owner.
:::

### Real estate

Property can bring **rent** and **price growth**, but it needs a lot of money, takes time to sell and has **legal risks** (land disputes, fake documents). Always **verify ownership** with a lawyer and official records before paying.

### Dollar and foreign assets

Some people hold foreign-currency assets to protect against a falling naira. These carry their own **risks and costs**, including exchange rate swings, fees and rules, so learn before you buy.

::: check What is a major risk in real estate buying?
+ Fake or disputed ownership documents
- It is always liquid
- It never needs checking
= Verify with a lawyer and official records.
:::

## 🧺 Diversification, time and costs

### Diversification: do not put all eggs in one basket

Spread your money across **different types of investments**, sectors and sometimes countries, so one failure does not wipe you out.

> 📌 **Remember:** diversification does not remove risk, but it lowers the damage from any single mistake.

::: check Why diversify?
+ So one bad investment cannot ruin everything
- To guarantee profit
- To avoid all risk
= It reduces concentration risk.
:::

### Time horizon

- **Under 3 years:** safer options.
- **3 to 7 years:** a careful mix.
- **7+ years:** can hold more growth assets, accepting ups and downs.

### Costs matter

Fees quietly eat returns. A 2% yearly fee on a 10% return leaves 8%. Check **management fees, transaction fees and taxes** before you invest.

::: check A fund returns 10% a year and charges a 2% fee. About what do you keep?
+ 8%
- 12%
- 10%
= Fees reduce your return.
:::

### Do not trade on emotion

Panic selling in a fall and buying because of hype are common beginner mistakes. A written plan helps you stay calm.

## 🔍 How to check if an investment is legitimate

Investment fraud is common. These checks protect you.

### Red flags

- **Guaranteed high returns** (for example "30% a month").
- Pressure to **"join now before it closes"**.
- Rewards for **recruiting others** (a sign of a pyramid or Ponzi scheme).
- You cannot clearly explain **where the profit comes from**.
- **Unregistered** operators, no office, only social media.
- Early payouts followed by trouble withdrawing.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** a **Ponzi scheme** pays old investors with new investors' money. It collapses when new money slows.

::: check Which is a Ponzi or pyramid sign?
+ Rewards for bringing in new investors, plus guaranteed returns
- A clear regulated product with fees explained
- A registered fund manager
= Profits come from recruiting, not real investing.
:::

### The checks

1. **Search the regulator's register.** In Nigeria, check the **SEC** for licensed operators, and whether the product is allowed.
2. Check the **company registration** and physical address.
3. Read **independent reviews**, and search the name plus "scam".
4. Ask **how profits are made**, and get **written terms**.
5. Start with a **small amount**, and test withdrawals.
6. Talk to someone you trust, and **never rush**.

::: check What is the first thing to check about an investment offer?
+ Whether the operator is registered with the regulator
- How many friends joined
- How quickly it pays
= Registration checks come first.
:::

### If you were scammed

Stop paying, keep all **evidence** (messages, receipts), report to your **bank**, the **police**, and the **regulator**, and warn others. Do not pay a "recovery agent" upfront, as that is often another scam.

::: check What should you not do after being scammed?
- Report it
+ Pay a stranger upfront to recover your money
- Keep evidence
= Recovery scams target victims.
:::

## 🗺️ Build your simple investing plan

### The plan, step by step

1. **Goal:** what is the money for, and by when?
2. **Time horizon:** how many years?
3. **Risk comfort:** how would you feel if it fell 20% for a while?
4. **Amount:** how much can you invest **regularly** without hurting your budget?
5. **Mix:** how will you spread it? (savings, T-bills, funds, shares)
6. **Rules:** when you will review, and what you will **not** do (borrow to invest, chase hype).

> 📌 **Remember:** small, regular investing for a long time beats waiting for the perfect moment.

::: check What is better for most beginners?
+ Small regular investing over a long time
- One huge risky bet
- Waiting for a tip
= Consistency and time are powerful.
:::

### Example (for learning only)

*Goal: 500,000 in 5 years for a business start. Horizon: 5 years. Risk comfort: low to medium. Plan: invest 6,000 a month in a mix of a money market fund and T-bills, review every 6 months, never borrow to invest.*

### Get help carefully

For big decisions, talk to a **licensed professional**. Be wary of anyone who is paid only if you buy, and never share your PIN or OTP.

::: check Who should you trust for big investment decisions?
+ A licensed professional who explains risks and fees
- Anyone promising profit
- A stranger on social media
= Licensing and clear explanations matter.
:::
