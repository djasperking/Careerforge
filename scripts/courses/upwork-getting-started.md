## 👋 Welcome: what Upwork is and what to expect

**Upwork** is a marketplace where businesses hire freelancers for projects and ongoing work: writing, design, admin support, programming, customer support, data entry, marketing and much more. You create a profile, apply to jobs with proposals, work with clients through the platform, and get paid through it.

### What you will learn

- 🧭 How Upwork works and how it makes money
- 🧱 Choosing a niche and building a profile
- 🔎 Finding jobs worth applying for
- ✍️ Writing proposals that get replies
- 🤝 Working with clients and getting reviews
- 🔒 Staying safe and keeping your account in good standing

> 📌 **Remember:** Upwork is competitive, especially for beginners. Success comes from a clear offer, good proposals, patience and doing excellent work, **not from shortcuts**.

### An honest note

- **No one can guarantee you work or income.** Many beginners send many proposals before their first job.
- **Rules, fees and features change.** Always check Upwork's current **Terms of Service**, **fees page** and **Help Centre**. This course explains the ideas and how to check the details.
- **Never pay anyone** to "get you a job", to sell you an account, or to "unlock" your profile. That is a scam or against the rules.

## ⚙️ How Upwork works

### The basic flow

1. **A client posts a job** (or invites you to one).
2. **Freelancers send proposals.** Each proposal usually costs **Connects**.
3. **The client chats with candidates** and hires one.
4. **A contract starts**, either **fixed price** (with milestones) or **hourly**.
5. **You deliver the work** and the client pays through Upwork.
6. **Both sides leave feedback.**

> 📌 **Remember:** keep communication and payments **inside Upwork**. That is how you get payment protection and avoid breaking the rules.

::: check Where should payments happen?
+ Inside Upwork
- On WhatsApp only
- In person only
= Taking payment off the platform can get accounts banned and removes protection.
:::

### Contract types

- **Fixed price:** one agreed price for a clear result, paid by **milestones**.
- **Hourly:** the client pays per hour tracked with Upwork's time tracker, which captures work evidence.

### Connects

**Connects** are the tokens you use to send proposals (and to boost them). You get some on a plan, and can buy more. Check the **current number of Connects a job needs** before applying, and plan your use carefully.

### Fees and getting paid

Upwork takes a **service fee** from what you earn, and withdrawal methods may have their own charges. **Check the current fee structure and the withdrawal options available in your country** in Upwork's Help Centre before planning your prices.

::: check Why check Upwork's current fees page?
+ Fees and rules change over time
- They never change
- They only matter to clients
= Plan your price using today's numbers.
:::

## 🎯 Choose your niche and service

### Beginner trap: "I can do everything"

Clients hire **specialists**. A specific offer gets more replies than a long list of unrelated services.

### Pick your niche with three questions

1. **What skill can I already do well?** (writing, spreadsheets, design, support, a language)
2. **Who needs it and pays for it?** (small businesses, coaches, online shops, agencies)
3. **Can I show proof?** (samples, projects, certificates)

### Turn it into a clear offer

> **"I help [type of client] to [result] with [service]."**
> *"I help online shops write product descriptions that sell."*
> *"I help coaches manage their inbox and calendar."*

> 📌 **Remember:** one clear niche, such as "product descriptions" or "inbox management", beats "writing and design and coding".

::: check Which offer is strongest for a beginner?
- I do everything
+ I help online shops write clear product descriptions
- I will do any job
= A specific offer is easier to trust and to find.
:::

### Check the market

Search Upwork for your service. Read **10 job posts**: what do clients ask for, and what do they pay? If almost nobody hires for it, reconsider the niche or **narrow** it.

::: check What should you do before settling on a niche?
+ Read real job posts to see what clients want and pay
- Guess
- Copy a random profile
= Research reduces wasted effort.
:::

## 🧱 Build a profile that gets approved and noticed

### Account basics (honest and simple)

- **Use your real name and real details.** You must verify identity.
- **One account per person.** Never share, sell or rent an account.
- Use **your own photo** (clear, friendly, plain background).
- Be truthful about your **location**.
- Be patient: **profile reviews can take time**, and not everyone is approved right away. If you are declined, read the reason, improve, and follow Upwork's current guidance. Do not try tricks.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** fake names, borrowed photos or disguised locations can lead to a permanent ban.

::: check Which is a safe approach to your Upwork identity?
+ Real name, real photo, one account that is yours
- A borrowed photo
- A second account to try again
= Honesty protects your account.
:::

### The parts of your profile

1. **Title:** the result you provide, such as *"Product description writer for online shops"*.
2. **Overview:** 4 to 6 short lines: who you help, how, your proof and what to do next. Start with the **client's** problem.
3. **Skills:** the ones people search for, matching your niche.
4. **Employment and education:** be honest and relevant.
5. **Portfolio:** 3 to 5 **best samples** with a one-line explanation.
6. **Rate:** consider your market and plan, using the current fee info.

> **Example overview opening:** *"Your product pages need to turn visitors into buyers. I write clear, friendly product descriptions that explain benefits and match your brand voice."*

### Make samples if you have no clients

Create **samples** for imaginary or friendly businesses: a few product descriptions, a content calendar, a sample report, a mock-up design. Never copy other people's work or claim others' work as yours.

::: check What should a beginner with no clients put in a portfolio?
+ Original samples that show how they work
- Other people's work
- Nothing
= Real samples build trust.
:::

## 🔎 Find good jobs and avoid bad ones

### Search smartly

- Search with **your niche keywords**.
- Use filters: **category, experience level, fixed or hourly, client history**.
- Sort by **newest**. **Early, relevant proposals** often get seen first.
- Save searches and check them daily.

### Read the client

Look at the job post and the client's profile:

- **Payment method verified?**
- **Spending history and reviews** from other freelancers.
- **A clear job description** with scope and budget.
- How **recently** it was posted, and how many proposals it has.

> 📌 **Remember:** apply to jobs you can **really do**, from clients who look serious. Quality beats volume.

::: check Which looks like a safer client?
+ Verified payment method, clear scope and good feedback
- No details, urgent, and asks to chat on WhatsApp
- Unverified payment and a vague request
= Clear, verified clients are safer.
:::

### Red flags

- Asks you to **work for free** or do a **big "test" job** unpaid.
- Wants to **move to WhatsApp, Telegram or email** before hiring.
- Asks for **your password, bank details, or ID** outside Upwork's process.
- Sends a **link or file** to "see the job details" (risk of malware).
- Offers **very high pay** for very easy work, or "guaranteed" results.
- Asks you to **pay money** or to buy something first.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** if a job feels wrong, **report it** and do not engage.

::: check A "client" asks you to chat on Telegram and pay a fee to start. What do you do?
+ Refuse and report it, because this is likely a scam
- Pay the fee
- Send your ID
= Stay on the platform, and never pay to work.
:::

## ✍️ Proposals that get replies

Most freelancers write about **themselves**. Winners write about the **client's problem**.

### A simple structure (under about 150 words)

1. **Hook:** show you read the post, with one sentence about their need.
2. **Solution:** how you will do it, in two or three steps.
3. **Proof:** one relevant sample or result (with a link).
4. **Question:** a smart, specific question that shows you thought about the job.
5. **Next step:** an easy call to action.

> **Example:**
> Hello Ms Bello, I read that you need 10 product descriptions for your handmade bags, in a warm tone for young buyers. I will write each at about 80 words, focus on benefits and materials, and use your keywords. Here is a sample for a similar shop: [link]. One question: do you want the care instructions in each description? I can start with two this week as a trial.

> 📌 **Remember:** talk about **their** problem first. Keep it short, specific and free of spelling errors.

::: check What should a proposal start with?
- A long story about you
+ A line showing you understood the client's need
- Your price only
= Lead with their need.
:::

### Mistakes to avoid

- Copy-pasting the same proposal everywhere.
- Writing "I am the best" with no proof.
- Ignoring the **screening questions** (answer every one).
- **Asking to chat off-platform** or sharing contact details early.
- Spelling errors.

### Pricing your proposal

- **Fixed price:** estimate the **hours** it takes, add a **buffer**, and set a price you can deliver at a fair rate, **after Upwork's fee**.
- **Hourly:** choose a rate that is fair for your skill level and the job.

Do not **undercharge so low** that you cannot afford to do good work. Do not overcharge with no proof either.

::: check What should you do with screening questions?
+ Answer each one clearly
- Skip them
- Paste the same answer to all
= They are how clients filter candidates.
:::

## 🤝 Interviews, contracts and delivering well

### When a client replies

- Reply **fast and politely**.
- Ask **questions** about the goal, the audience, deadline and examples.
- Confirm **scope, price, deadline and number of revisions** in writing.
- Never promise what you cannot deliver.

> 📌 **Remember:** a clear scope in writing prevents most disputes.

::: check What should you confirm before starting?
+ Scope, price, deadline and revisions
- Nothing
- Only the colour
= Clarity protects both sides.
:::

### Fixed-price milestones

Break big jobs into **milestones**. The client funds a milestone **before** you start it, and releases it when you deliver. Do **not** start working on a milestone that has not been funded.

### Delivering like a pro

1. **Start with an update** within a day.
2. **Share progress early** (a draft or first section).
3. **Deliver on time**, or tell the client early if you need more.
4. **Proofread** and check the brief again.
5. **Ask for feedback**, and fix issues politely.

### Reviews and your reputation

Your **reviews and success measures** help you win more work. Aim for **excellent work, clear communication and honest expectations**. Never ask a client to leave a fake review or to trade reviews.

::: check Should you start work on an unfunded milestone?
- Yes, to be nice
+ No, wait until it is funded
- Only for new clients
= Funded milestones protect your payment.
:::

## 🔒 Staying safe and keeping your account in good standing

### Rules that protect your account

- **Keep communication and payments on Upwork.**
- **One account**, in your real name, with your own details.
- **Do not share** your password or let others use your account.
- Do not **buy reviews or ask for fake ones**.
- Do not **copy work** or claim others' work as yours.
- Follow the **Terms of Service**, and re-check them regularly.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** violating rules can lead to a permanent ban, and lost earnings.

### Protect yourself from scams

- Never **pay to get a job** or to unlock features.
- Do not open **unknown links or files** from clients. Use trusted tools.
- Never give **bank PIN, OTP or password** to anyone.
- Be careful with **overpayment scams** and **fake cheque** stories.
- Use **two-step verification** on your Upwork, email and bank accounts.

::: check Which of these is a rule violation risk?
+ Taking a client's payment outside Upwork
- Messaging within Upwork
- Using your real name
= It removes protection and can get you banned.
:::

### If something goes wrong

- **Document everything** (messages, files, dates).
- Use **Upwork's dispute and support options** and follow their steps.
- Stay **professional**. Do not argue publicly.

### Taxes and records

Keep records of your earnings. **Check the tax rules in your country** or talk to an accountant, because this course is general education.

::: check Where should you do disputes and support?
+ Through Upwork's official dispute and support options
- Public social media posts
- By threatening the client
= Keep it official and professional.
:::
