## 🧠 What is data annotation, really?

### How AI learns

An AI model learns by looking at **millions of examples**. If you want it to recognise a cat, you show it many photos, each **labelled** "cat" or "not cat". The labels are the teacher.

> 📌 **Remember:** labelled examples are how AI learns. The labels are written by people.

::: check What is a label?
+ A tag that tells the AI what an example is
- A sticker on the computer
- A type of computer program
= Labels are the correct answers AI studies.
:::

### Types of annotation work

- **Classification:** is this email spam or not? is this review positive, negative or neutral?
- **Bounding boxes and tagging:** drawing a box around cars in a photo.
- **Transcription:** typing out what is said in an audio clip.
- **Ranking:** which of two AI answers is better, and why?
- **Writing:** writing example questions and ideal answers for a chatbot.
- **Review and moderation:** checking content for safety and quality.

::: check Which task is ranking?
- Typing out an audio recording
+ Choosing which of two AI answers is better
- Drawing a box around a car
= Ranking means comparing outputs and judging quality.
:::

### Try it: classify these sentences

Read each line and decide: **positive**, **negative** or **neutral**.

1. "The delivery arrived on time and the food was hot."
2. "I waited two hours and nobody replied."
3. "The shop opens at 9am."

Answers: 1 is positive, 2 is negative, 3 is neutral (it states a fact, with no feeling).

::: check "The shop opens at 9am." is best labelled as...
- Positive
- Negative
+ Neutral
= It is a fact with no opinion or feeling.
:::

> 💡 **Tip:** when unsure, ask yourself: *what would a careful reader of the guidelines choose?*

## 🔍 The skills that matter most

You do not need a degree. You need these habits.

### 1. Reading carefully

Every project comes with **guidelines**, a document that explains exactly how to label. People who read them fully, and again when unsure, score higher than fast readers.

> 📌 **Remember:** the guidelines are the boss. Your opinion matters less than the rules.

::: check A task says "label sarcasm as negative". You think it is funny. What do you do?
- Label it funny
+ Follow the guideline and label it negative
- Skip the task
= Annotators must apply the project's rules, not personal taste.
:::

### 2. Consistency

Label the same kind of thing the **same way every time**. Models learn patterns, and inconsistent labels confuse them.

### 3. Attention to detail

A missed word, a box slightly too big, a wrong tag: small errors add up across thousands of tasks.

### 4. Clear writing

Writing and ranking tasks need correct grammar, clear thinking and honesty about what you do not know.

### 5. Staying focused

Tasks can be repetitive. Short breaks, a quiet place and a routine protect your accuracy.

::: check Why does consistency matter?
+ Models learn patterns, so inconsistent labels confuse them
- It makes the work look neat
- It is only for beginners
= Reliable labels give better training data.
:::

> 💡 **Tip:** accuracy first, speed second. Fast and wrong costs you more than slow and right.

## 📘 Following guidelines

### Read, then read again

A guideline usually contains: the **goal**, **definitions**, **examples**, **edge cases** and **what to do when unsure**. Read it all before you start and keep it open while you work.

> 📌 **Remember:** when a task feels unclear, go back to the guidelines before you guess.

::: check What should you do first when a task feels unclear?
- Guess quickly
+ Check the guidelines and their examples
- Ask a friend to do it
= The answer is usually in the guidelines.
:::

### Practice: apply a rule

**Guideline:** *Label a message as TOXIC only if it insults a person or group. Strong opinions, swearing at a situation, or criticism are NOT toxic.*

Messages:

1. "You are an idiot and nobody likes you."
2. "This weather is rubbish!"
3. "I disagree with the new price, it is too high."

Answers: 1 is TOXIC (insults a person). 2 and 3 are NOT toxic.

::: check Under that guideline, "This weather is rubbish!" is...
+ Not toxic
- Toxic
- Impossible to label
= It complains about a situation and does not insult a person.
:::

### Edge cases and ambiguity

Sometimes an example fits two labels or none. Good annotators:

1. Look for the guideline's **edge-case section**.
2. Choose the **most reasonable** reading.
3. Use the **unsure / flag** option if the platform has one, instead of guessing.

> 💡 **Tip:** keep a short personal notes file of tricky cases you have solved. It makes you faster and more consistent.

::: check If a task fits no label and you cannot tell, the best move is...
- Pick any label
+ Use the flag or unsure option, or check the edge-case notes
- Delete the task
= Flagging helps the team fix unclear guidelines.
:::

## 🎯 Quality, accuracy and scores

### How quality is measured

Companies check your work in several ways:

- **Gold tasks:** hidden questions with known answers. They measure your accuracy.
- **Reviews:** a senior reviewer checks a sample of your work.
- **Agreement:** how often your answers match other annotators.

> 📌 **Remember:** treat every task as if it will be checked, because some will be.

::: check What are gold tasks?
+ Hidden tasks with known correct answers that test your accuracy
- Tasks that pay extra gold
- Practice tasks you can skip
= They are how quality is checked quietly.
:::

### The common mistakes

1. **Rushing:** skimming the instructions.
2. **Guessing:** choosing without understanding.
3. **Letting opinions in:** labelling what you believe is right instead of what the guideline says.
4. **Copying AI answers** into tasks that must be your own work. This can get you removed.
5. **Inconsistency** between similar tasks.

::: check Which of these can get you removed from a project?
- Taking a short break
+ Pasting AI-generated text into a task that must be your own work
- Reading the guidelines twice
= Many projects ban AI-written answers when they are asking for human judgment.
:::

### Speed versus accuracy

Start slow and **accurate**. Speed comes naturally with practice, so do not chase it early.

```text
Accuracy first  →  consistency  →  then speed
```

> 💡 **Tip:** a good rhythm is about 25 minutes of work and a 5-minute break.

::: check Which order is best for beginners?
- Speed, then accuracy
+ Accuracy, then consistency, then speed
- Neither matters
= Fast but wrong work lowers your score and can end your access.
:::

## ✍️ Writing and ranking tasks

Many AI training jobs are not clicking, but **thinking**: comparing AI answers and writing better ones.

### Ranking two answers

You will often see a question and two AI replies, and judge which is better. Look at:

- **Correctness:** are the facts right?
- **Helpfulness:** does it answer what was asked?
- **Clarity:** is it easy to understand?
- **Safety:** does it avoid harm or rude language?

> 📌 **Remember:** judge correct, helpful, clear and safe. A long answer is not automatically a better one.

::: check Two answers: one is long and wrong, one is short and correct. Which is better?
- The long one
+ The short, correct one
- They are equal
= Correctness comes first, and length does not make it better.
:::

### Giving your reason

Often you must write a short reason. A good reason is specific:

- **Weak:** "B is better."
- **Strong:** "B is better because it gives the correct date and explains the steps. A has the wrong date."

::: check Which reason is strongest?
- "I like it more."
+ "B is better because it is correct and answers the full question."
- "It looks nicer."
= Specific, factual reasons are what reviewers look for.
:::

### Writing examples for a chatbot

Some tasks ask you to write a question and an ideal answer. Good ideal answers are accurate, clear, polite, and honest about what is unknown.

> 💡 **Tip:** if you are not sure a fact is true, check a reliable source before you write it. Never invent facts.

::: check What should you do if you are unsure about a fact you want to include?
- Include it anyway
+ Check a reliable source or leave it out
- Make up a number that sounds right
= Wrong facts in training data make AI worse.
:::

## 🔎 Finding real jobs and avoiding scams

### Where the work comes from

- **AI and data companies** that hire annotators directly or through contractor programmes.
- **Marketplaces and platforms** that connect workers to projects.
- **Agencies** that supply workers to large projects.
- **Job boards**, like the Career Forge **Jobs** page, which links to the employer's own application page.

> 📌 **Remember:** always apply on the employer's own website, not through a stranger's message.

::: check Where should you submit your application?
- To a stranger on WhatsApp who sends you a link
+ On the employer's own official website
- Anywhere that is fastest
= Check you are on the real site before entering any details.
:::

### Scam red flags

- They ask you to **pay** a fee, deposit or "training cost".
- They promise **guaranteed income** or "earn 50,000 a day".
- They contact you first on WhatsApp or Telegram and want to **move off the platform**.
- They send you a **cheque** and ask you to send some money back.
- They ask for your **bank PIN, password or OTP**.
- The email comes from a free address that does not match the company name.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** real companies never need your PIN, password or an OTP code.

::: check Which is a clear scam sign?
- A job description that lists skills needed
+ A request to send a fee to get started
- An application form on the company website
= Never pay to get paid.
:::

### Check before you trust

1. Search the company name plus "scam" or "reviews".
2. Check the website address spelling and look for a real address and support page.
3. Ask a friend you trust to look at the offer too.

::: check What should you do before trusting a new company?
- Send your ID immediately
+ Research the company and check reviews
- Share the offer in a group chat
= A few minutes of checking can save you a lot.
:::

## 📝 Applications, assessments and onboarding

### What the process usually looks like

1. **Application:** a form, a short CV and sometimes your location and language skills.
2. **Assessment:** a timed test of reading, attention to detail and following guidelines.
3. **Onboarding:** training, guidelines, and a few practice tasks.
4. **Work:** tasks start flowing, and quality is monitored.

> 📌 **Remember:** the assessment tests carefulness more than speed. Read everything.

::: check What do assessments mainly test?
- How fast you can click
+ How carefully you read and follow instructions
- How many languages you speak
= Careful reading and reasoning is what they look for.
:::

### Preparing for an assessment

- Use a **laptop or desktop** if possible, with stable internet and charged power.
- Choose a **quiet time** when you will not be interrupted.
- **Read each instruction twice.** Look at the examples.
- Do not use AI tools to answer unless it is explicitly allowed.
- Keep a pen and paper to note rules.

::: check What should you do with the examples in the instructions?
- Skip them
+ Study them closely and match your answers to them
- Ignore them if you are in a hurry
= Examples show exactly what the company wants.
:::

### Writing a good application

- Keep your CV **one page**, with your skills, languages and any relevant experience.
- Show **attention to detail**: no spelling mistakes!
- Be honest about your skills and availability.
- Use the Career Forge **CV Builder** to make a clean CV.

> 💡 **Tip:** mention strengths like careful reading, consistency, English fluency and reliability.

::: check Which belongs on an annotation CV?
- A very long life story
+ Clear skills like careful reading, English and reliability
- A false claim of experience
= Be honest and specific.
:::

## 💻 Setting up to work remotely

### Your setup

- **Device:** a laptop or desktop with a working keyboard and a decent screen.
- **Internet:** a reliable connection and a backup (such as mobile data).
- **Power:** a plan for outages: a charged laptop, power bank or inverter.
- **Space:** a quiet corner, even a small one.

> 📌 **Remember:** a stable setup protects your work. Plan for power and data before tasks begin.

::: check Why plan for power and internet cuts?
+ Lost connection mid-task can lose work or lower your score
- It makes the room look nicer
- It is only needed on weekends
= Reliability matters to employers.
:::

### Routine and focus

1. Work in **blocks** (for example 25 minutes) with short breaks.
2. Keep **notes** of rules and tricky cases beside you.
3. Silence notifications while you work.
4. Stop when you are tired. Mistakes cost more than breaks.

### Protecting yourself

- Use a **strong, different password** and turn on two-step verification.
- Keep work accounts **separate** from personal ones.
- Never share your login. It can get you banned.
- Rest your eyes and take care of your posture.

### Getting paid

- Read how and **when** you are paid before you start.
- Keep records of your tasks and payments.
- Learn the basics of **tax** for freelancers in your country.
- Expect that **work volume can change**, so build income from more than one source.

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** do not depend on one platform for everything. Projects end.

::: check Why should you not rely on a single source of work?
- Platforms are all bad
+ Projects can end or slow down, so income can change
- It is against the rules to have more than one
= Variety protects you.
:::
